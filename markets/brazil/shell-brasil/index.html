Brazil · Shell Brasil

Explosion proof mobile phone for Shell Brasil, Santos Basin pre-salt

An explosion proof mobile phone for Shell Brasil has to suit offshore work in Brazil's deep water. Shell holds interests in the Santos Basin pre-salt, including the Mero and Atapu developments, and operates the Parque das Conchas fields in the Campos Basin. This page is for the HSE, telecoms and operations teams who decide what may be carried onto a floating production unit and across a classified boundary. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

The sites · Shell Brasil

Explosion proof mobile phone use across Shell's Brazil portfolio

Shell's Brazilian upstream is mostly non-operated: in the pre-salt it holds participating interests alongside Petrobras and other partners, and the units are run to the operator's procedures. Parque das Conchas, in the Campos Basin, is the field where Shell is the operator, so its own procedures apply. Shell has also been developing Gato do Mato, a Santos Basin discovery planned for later this decade. A handset therefore meets several rulebooks in a single career: the operator's on a partner-run FPSO, and Shell's own where it controls operations.

What a pre-salt rotation does to a handset

The classification sets the limit. The rotation, the offshore environment and the number of parties involved set the day-to-day experience.

A rotation, not a shift

A technician flies out, works the rotation and flies back. If a handset fails mid-rotation there is no replacement until the next helicopter, so reliability and sealing matter more than headline features. The 4,000 mAh cell is meant to carry a working day between charges in the accommodation.

Salt, spray and constant motion

A deck environment is wet and salty. IP68 sealing and the rugged build are the starting point, and drop resistance matters when the user is wearing gloves and the platform is moving.

Several parties on one unit

Owner of the vessel, operator, shuttle-tanker crews and contractors all have rules. A device enrolled in an existing MDM console can carry a profile for each, so the Shell team can restrict what runs on its own users' units.

Marking that reads in ATEX and IECEx

A European operator's engineers usually reference both. The IS-04Z1 is being certified to IECEx and ATEX, with a marking target of II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4, but the certificates will define scope once they exist.

Portuguese for the crew, English for the review

Brazilian crews work in Portuguese, while a global operator's review is often in English. The interface runs in Brazilian Portuguese and the documentation is intended in both languages.

Questions · Shell Brasil

Frequently asked questions

Is the IS-04Z1 certified for use at Shell Brasil sites yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and certification, including ATEX and IECEx, is in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch. No unit should be taken into a classified area before the relevant certificates exist.

Who decides what is allowed on a partner-operated FPSO?

The operator of the field sets the site rules, and the vessel owner may add its own. A non-operating partner such as Shell would normally have to work within them, so ask the operator's HSE team first.

Does the high CO2 content of pre-salt gas change the classification?

It adds treatment and compression equipment, and so classified space, but the hazardous-area classification is decided from the gas and release sources on each unit. The site's classification drawing is the document to check.

Is Shell Brasil the same as Raízen?

No. Raízen is a separate company, a joint venture of Shell and Cosan, with its own mills, safety procedures and procurement.
