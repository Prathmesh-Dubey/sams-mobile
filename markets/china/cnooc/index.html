The sites · CNOOC

Where an explosion proof mobile phone is needed on CNOOC's platforms and terminals in China

Offshore work splits into two basins with different problems. In the central Bohai Sea, the Bozhong 19-6 condensate gas field is reported by trade press to be developed with one central processing platform, three unmanned wellhead platforms and a gas processing terminal onshore. Bohai is shallow and partly freezes in winter, so platforms are built to resist ice. In the South China Sea, CNOOC's Lingshui 17-2 deepwater gas project is produced through Shenhai-1, a 53,000 tonne semi-submersible production and storage platform in about 1,500 metres of water. Between these sit older fixed platforms and floating production units. Onshore, gas arrives at terminals, and CNOOC is also present in refining and petrochemicals at Huizhou in Guangdong. Offshore areas are Zone 1 and Zone 2 gas almost everywhere processing happens, with drilling, wellhead and compression modules the most heavily classified.

What the sea does to a handset

Salt, motion and transfer logistics shape an offshore handset more than any single hazard.

Salt air and spray

Chloride corrodes ports and seams over months. IP68 sealing is the design starting point, and long-term salt exposure is something to raise with us during evaluation.

Winter ice in Bohai

Seasonal ice affects access and transfers in the north. Cold also shortens battery life, so the certificate's temperature range is the figure to check, not a general claim.

Helicopter and vessel transfers

Personnel travel offshore by helicopter or vessel. Carriage rules for lithium batteries and electronic devices are set by the operator and aviation provider, so ask them before shipping units.

Unmanned platforms visited by crews

A visit to an unmanned wellhead platform is short and planned. A handset with GNSS, a 50 MP camera and a 4,000 mAh battery carries the permit and records the visit without a safe-area stop.

Network depends on the platform

Connectivity offshore is set by the platform's own network, not the handset. The device is managed from existing MDM consoles, so policy follows it whichever network is available.

How work is organised

Offshore access, partners and approvals at CNOOC

Offshore access begins long before the platform: survival training, medicals and a permit to travel. Crews work set rotations, and maintenance campaigns are packed into planned shutdown windows because vessel and helicopter capacity is limited. Fields developed with international partners can carry more than one set of engineering expectations, so a supplier may be asked for IECEx or ATEX evidence alongside the domestic route. We have not verified a published supplier-registration programme at CNOOC and do not name one. Ask the operator's procurement and HSE teams which Ex certificates and test reports they accept for a specific platform. NEPSI is among the certification routes in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Frequently asked questions

Does a partner's IECEx or ATEX requirement replace NEPSI offshore?

Not by itself. Partner standards and the domestic route can both apply to one field, and the operator's engineers decide which evidence they accept. Ask early which documents a specific platform requires.

Can the handset be carried on helicopter transfers?

That is an operator and aviation provider rule, covering lithium batteries and electronic devices. Ask for the current carriage policy before planning shipments, and do not assume a certified device is exempt.

Is a handset enough for communication on an unmanned platform?

Only if the platform has a network. The handset works on whatever the platform provides, and the operator's telecoms team decides coverage.

Is the IS-04Z1 certified for CNOOC platforms yet?

No. It is pre-launch, certification is in progress and the Q4 2026 launch follows the certificates. Nothing should go into an offshore classified area before then.
