The company · PETRONAS Chemicals Group

Explosion proof mobile phone for PETRONAS Chemicals Group, Malaysia

The group describes itself as one of the largest integrated chemicals producers in Southeast Asia, with 19 manufacturing sites and 30 production plants worldwide and a combined capacity of 15.4 million tonnes a year. In Malaysia its plants fall into clear families. At Kerteh in Terengganu it makes olefins, glycols, derivatives, ethylene, polyethylene, ammonia, aromatics, vinyl chloride and acrylic acid. At Gebeng in Pahang it makes polypropylene, MTBE, 2-EHA and silicone blends. Gurun in Kedah and Sipitang in Sabah make ammonia and urea, Labuan produces methanol and Pengerang adds polyethylene, polypropylene, ethylene glycols and nitrile butadiene latex. The group works through joint ventures including BASF PETRONAS Chemicals and LG PETRONAS Chemicals Malaysia. That variety means no single handset configuration fits every plant: a methanol plant is mostly flammable liquid and vapour, a fertiliser plant is ammonia and dust, and a polymer plant has Zone 21 powder.

What PETRONAS Chemicals plants do to an explosion proof phone

Chemical plants ask different things of a handset than refineries. These are the points worth raising with a plant's HSE and telecoms teams.

Ammonia first, ignition second

At an ammonia plant the first alarm is usually a toxic gas detector, and an intrinsically safe handset does not replace one. It does help the response: an operator can report a leak position by GNSS and photograph the area from a safe distance, while breathing apparatus and gas detection remain the primary protection.

Methanol flames you cannot see

Methanol burns with a flame that is hard to see in daylight, so plants rely on detectors and procedures. A phone certified for Zone 1 lets operators raise the alarm from the area itself. Check the gas group, since methanol sits in IIA, and the temperature class on the certificate.

Powder in fertiliser and polymer plants

Zone 21 applies where combustible dust may form a cloud. Whether it applies to a urea store is for the site's dust study, but polymer silos and bagging halls are a likelier case. The IS-04Z1's Zone 21 design target is relevant to the second.

Four regions, one handset policy

Plants in Terengganu, Pahang, Kedah, Sabah, Labuan and Johor share group IT and HSE standards. A single device on Android Enterprise, managed from the existing MDM console, can be rolled out in the same configuration to every plant.

English and Bahasa Malaysia

Operators, shift supervisors and visiting contractors may prefer either language. The interface runs in both on one hardware build, and a 50 MP camera records a defect clearly in tropical light.

Frequently asked questions

Does PETRONAS Chemicals Group have its own approved-equipment list?

Large chemical groups usually run vendor registration and technical standards of their own, which apply on top of national certification. We cannot verify a specific programme, so ask the plant's procurement and HSE teams what registration, testing and induction are required. SAMS has no relationship with the group.

Which sites are covered by this page?

The plants named on the group's public site, in Kerteh, Gebeng, Gurun, Sipitang, Labuan and Pengerang. Joint ventures such as BASF PETRONAS Chemicals and LG PETRONAS Chemicals Malaysia may have their own site rules. The petrochemical sector page covers the wider clusters, and the PETRONAS page covers the parent.

Can the handset be used at an ammonia plant?

Only where the zone and gas group on the plant drawings allow it and the certificate exists. Ammonia has a high ignition temperature and a narrow flammable range, and it is mostly a toxic hazard, so personal gas detectors remain essential. The handset adds communication and recording.

Is the Pengerang polymer complex part of this group?

The group lists polyethylene, polypropylene, glycols and latex at Pengerang. The refinery and cracker there belong to a separate venture, described on the Pengerang Refining and Petrochemical page.
