Explosion proof mobile phone for refining and chemicals in Canada

Canada’s refining and chemicals sector is split across Sarnia, Edmonton and Fort Saskatchewan, Saint John and the St Lawrence, and an explosion proof mobile phone for Canada has to work in refinery units and polymer plants alike. This page is for the HSE, turnaround and telecoms staff who specify equipment for those regions. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

The sector · Refining and chemicals

What is classified in each region

Sarnia’s Chemical Valley holds Imperial’s refinery, a Suncor refinery, NOVA Chemicals’ Corunna site and other plants, packed along the St Clair River. In Alberta, Imperial’s Strathcona and Suncor’s Edmonton refineries and Shell’s Scotford complex sit near Edmonton and Fort Saskatchewan, with Dow’s Fort Saskatchewan site and NOVA’s Joffre ethylene plant in the same corridor. On the Atlantic, Irving Oil’s Saint John refinery, the largest in Canada, receives crude at Canaport. In Quebec, Valero’s Jean Gaulin refinery at Lévis and Suncor’s Montréal refinery serve the St Lawrence.

A refinery is classified around distillation, conversion units, tank farms and loading racks, with hydrogen at hydrotreaters. A cracker adds ethylene, and polyethylene plants add powder and pellet dust, so Zone 21 matters as much as Zone 1. Contractors move between neighbouring plants, so one handset policy must hold across sites with separate owners. Edmonton’s Heartland has a different feel again: large plants spread over open farmland, where a round can involve a drive between units and the wind and winter cold matter as much as the hazard class.

Hydrogen and ethylene

Hydrotreaters carry hydrogen and crackers carry ethylene. The IS-04Z1 is designed to a Zone 1 IIC target, intended to cover the range of gas groups in one device.

Dust in polymer plants

Powder and pellet handling is a dust hazard. A Zone 21 design target means one device is meant to suit both gas and dust units.

Both official languages

Android 16 runs in English and French, which matters for crews in Quebec and for contractors moving between provinces.

Many owners, one cluster

Sarnia contractors work for several operators in a week. Each operator manages enrolment under its own policy, and a handset is wiped at the end of each job.

Turnaround seasons and provincial rules

Refining is organised around planned shutdowns and by province.

Planned shutdowns bring large contractor crews into units on a schedule. Turnaround seasons at neighbouring plants compete for the same trades, so handsets are issued, enrolled and returned in large batches.

Questions · Refining and chemicals

Frequently asked questions

Does approval at one Sarnia plant carry to its neighbour?

No. Each operator runs its own review and procurement, even where sites are adjacent, and an approval at one plant is not a reference the next plant is obliged to accept.

Which gas groups are present?

Hydrogen is IIC, ethylene is IIB, and most refinery hydrocarbons are IIA. A device designed to IIC covers the range.

Is CSA certification the only requirement?

Canadian code requires certification by a Standards Council of Canada accredited body. IECEx is supporting evidence.

Does the device cover polymer plants?

It is designed for Zone 21 dust as well as Zone 1 gas, subject to each site’s area drawings. Polyethylene powder and pellet areas are where that second half of the design target matters most.
