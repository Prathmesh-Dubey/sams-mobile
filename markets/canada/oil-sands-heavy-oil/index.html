Explosion proof mobile phone for oil sands and heavy oil in Canada

Alberta’s oil sands are produced by open-pit mining north of Fort McMurray and by steam wells at Cold Lake, Christina Lake and Firebag, and an explosion proof mobile phone for oil sands in Canada has to cope with both, plus the upgraders and the heavy oil fields around Lloydminster. This page is for the HSE, operations and telecoms staff who write equipment standards for those sites. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

The sector · Oil sands

What is classified from the mine face to the upgrader

In the Athabasca region, mining operations include Suncor’s Base Plant and Fort Hills, Syncrude’s Mildred Lake and Aurora North, Imperial’s Kearl, and CNRL’s Horizon and Albian mines. In situ operators produce with steam from well pads: Suncor at Firebag and MacKay River, Cenovus at Foster Creek and Christina Lake, Imperial at Cold Lake and CNRL at Jackfish and Primrose. Upgraders at Base Plant, Mildred Lake, Horizon and Scotford turn bitumen into synthetic crude.

The process defines the hazards. Mines have froth treatment with solvent and tank farms; in situ plants have steam generators and gas treating; upgraders have cokers, hydrotreaters and hydrogen systems, plus coke and sulphur dust. Heavy oil adds a third pattern around Lloydminster, on the Alberta and Saskatchewan border, where thermal and cold-production wells feed upgrading and refining. The mine pit and haul road are mostly outside the classified boundary, but the shops, extraction plants and pump houses beside them are not.

The oil sands are hard on equipment for reasons that have little to do with classification.

Cold that outlasts the shift

Winter cold at Fort McMurray goes far below zero. The certificate states the ambient range, and electrical inspectors read that line. Battery capacity in the cold deserves its own test before a fleet is bought.

Gas and dust in one plant

The IS-04Z1 is designed for Zone 1 gas and Zone 21 dust, so one handset is intended to cover froth treatment as well as coke and sulphur handling.

Gloves and one free hand

Winter gloves and FR coveralls leave little room for a notebook. A 50 MP camera and a managed device let an operator capture a defect without taking gloves off to write. Whether a given glove and the touchscreen work together is worth testing in an evaluation, in the same cold as the job.

Large footprints, few landmarks

Four-constellation GNSS puts a coordinate on a report across a site measured in kilometres.

Questions · Oil sands

Frequently asked questions

Is cold the main problem?

It is one of them. The certificate states a marked ambient range, which is published when the certificate issues, so check it against the coldest duty before purchase, and test battery runtime in the cold as part of any evaluation.

Are mines classified?

Not the pit or haul road. The extraction plant, tanks and upgrader are, so the answer is by area.

Do in situ plants differ from mines?

Yes. In situ plants have smaller classified areas spread across many pads, while mines concentrate them in extraction plants and upgraders. The pad round is a driving job and the plant round is a walking one, so battery and navigation needs differ.

Is IECEx enough?

It is useful supporting evidence, but Canada expects certification from a Standards Council of Canada accredited body.
