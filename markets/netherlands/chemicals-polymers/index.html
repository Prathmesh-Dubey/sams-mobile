Explosion proof mobile phone for Chemicals and Polymers in the Netherlands

Dutch chemical production sits on four main sites: Chemelot at Geleen in Limburg, Moerdijk on the Rotterdam to Antwerp corridor, Terneuzen and the Sloe area in Zeeland. This page is for HSE officers, instrumentation engineers and contractors who need an explosion proof mobile phone for chemicals and polymers sites in the Netherlands, where flammable gas and combustible dust often share the same fence. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

The sector · Dutch chemical parks

Where chemicals and polymers are classified in the Netherlands

A chemical park in the Netherlands is usually a fenced estate of separate companies sharing utilities, roads and gate rules. Chemelot in Geleen is the clearest example, with a site-services company, Sitech, looking after maintenance and facilities for the park. At Terneuzen the anchor is Dow’s integrated complex, covered on our explosion proof phone for Dow Benelux page, and at Moerdijk Shell runs a chemical site linked to its Pernis refinery, covered on the page for Shell Nederland. The common thread is that gas, liquid and powder processing are all present, so the zoning mixes Zone 1 or 2 for flammable gas and vapour with Zone 21 or 22 for combustible dust.

Gas next to dust

The sector’s defining zoning problem is a solvent area and a powder area within a few hundred metres. A Zone 1 and Zone 21 design target means one handset, with the marking on the certificate confirming where it may go.

Seveso status is routine

Most large parks are major-hazard sites. Since 1 January 2024 the Seveso rules sit within the Omgevingswet, but labour inspectorate, environment service and safety region still read the same equipment file.

Contractors move between companies

A maintenance firm may work at Chemelot one month and Terneuzen the next. A handset that can be enrolled in the host’s console and wiped afterwards is easier to admit than a personal device.

Camera policy

Photography is restricted on most chemical sites. Android Enterprise lets the operator set camera policy centrally, so the device can be shown to comply.

Questions · Chemicals and Polymers

Frequently asked questions

Why does dust matter for a phone on a chemical site?

Powders such as polymer or fertiliser can form explosive clouds. Zone 21 equipment must be protected against them, which is why dust is part of the programme and not an afterthought.

Does the same phone work in a solvent area and a powder area?

That is the design aim. The certificate shows the scope, and the site’s zoning decides where it is allowed.

Do chemical parks share one approval process?

Not that we have verified. Each company usually sets its own rules, even when the gate is shared. Ask each operator.
