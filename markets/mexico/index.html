The Division system, written in Spanish

NOM-001-SEDE follows the American structure: Article 500 classifies áreas peligrosas as Clase I, II and III with Divisiones 1 and 2. The device is designed for that duty and for Zone 1, so one specification answers a drawing written either way.

Gulf coast heat and salt air

Altamira, Coatzacoalcos, Dos Bocas and a Campeche deck combine high humidity with salt-laden air and long hours outdoors. IP68 sealing and rugged build cover the physical side, and T4 is reconciled with the drawing.

Dust areas that get left out

Coke handling at a refinery, sulfur, cement, grain and mineral processing are combustible dust locations where a gas-only fleet stops. The Zone 21 design target keeps one device valid across both.

Offshore rotations and boat transfers

A Campeche platform is reached by helicopter or crew boat, and a failed handset waits for the next one. A 4,000 mAh battery, a Snapdragon octa-core platform, four-constellation GNSS and platform Wi-Fi suit that pattern.

Spanish from the first screen

Android 16 runs the interface in Spanish, and Spanish documentation is part of the launch package. It matters for hazard communication under NOM-018-STPS and for training records an inspector expects in the working language.

Contractor fleets that turn over fast

Refinery and offshore scopes are staffed by crews that change with the contract. EMM and MDM enrollment locks a device to an approved app set and wipes it at demobilization, so a loaned fleet stays accounted for.

Regulatory route · Mexico

What your HSE and procurement teams will ask for.

The standard your electrical designer works from is NOM-001-SEDE, the Mexican official standard for electrical installations, and it is compulsory. Article 500 classifies áreas peligrosas as Clase I for flammable gases and vapours, Clase II for combustible dust and Clase III for fibres, each with Divisiones 1 and 2, and the articles after it set what equipment may be used there. The structure mirrors the American code, which is why a Mexican site asks for Clase I División 1 even where the plant was engineered to an international standard and the same area would be called Zone 1. Conformity is not self-declared: an installation in a classified area is assessed by a unidad de verificación accredited for the standard, and the dictamen it issues is what the plant produces when inspected.

Three further obligations shape the purchase. In hydrocarbons the safety requirements sit with ASEA, the Agencia Nacional de Seguridad Industrial y de Protección al Medio Ambiente del Sector Hidrocarburos, which regulates operations from wellhead to retail. On the workplace side NOM-005-STPS governs work with hazardous chemical substances and NOM-018-STPS governs hazard identification and communication, and together they are where a site records what may be carried past a boundary sign. On the radio side, no telecommunications product may be sold or connected without homologación, and that responsibility changed hands recently: the telecommunications law of 2025 dissolved the IFT and moved type approval to the Comisión Reguladora de Telecomunicaciones in October 2025, while certificates already issued and the IFT number on existing labels remain valid.

Is the IS-04Z1 certified for Clase I División 1 in Mexico today?

No. It is designed for Clase I División 1 and Zone 1 duty and certification is in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch. Until the certificate exists no Mexican site should treat it as suitable for a classified area.

Our plant accepts equipment listed in the United States. Does that carry into Mexico?

Partly, and it is worth settling early. NOM-001-SEDE classifies areas the same way, so the Clase I División 1 description applies, but the acceptance decision is made by the unidad de verificación. Agree in advance which certificates go into the dictamen file.

Is this a flameproof phone, or an intrinsically safe one?

Buyers ask for a flameproof or a prueba de explosión phone and mean any handset usable in a classified area, but the concepts differ. Flameproof, Ex d, contains an ignition inside a heavy enclosure. A handheld takes the seguridad intrínseca route, Ex ib.

Does it need homologación, and who issues that now?

Yes, and the number appears on the product. Type approval moved from the IFT to the Comisión Reguladora de Telecomunicaciones in October 2025 under the new telecommunications law, while certificates the IFT already issued remain valid, so check which body issued the one you are shown.

Will the interface and documentation be in Spanish?

Android 16 runs the interface in Spanish, and Spanish documentation is part of the launch package. Ask for the manual and label text with the certification schedule so your NOM-018-STPS material is ready alongside the rollout.

What is the price of an explosion proof phone in Mexico?

There is no list price before certification is complete. Pre-launch pricing is given on request once we know fleet size, configuration and whether supply runs through a Mexican distributor or direct, because the import route changes the landed figure.

Can it be used offshore in the Bay of Campeche?

It is designed for that duty: Clase I División 1 and Zone 1 gas areas, IP68 and rugged build, and platform Wi-Fi where cellular coverage stops. Confirm the operator policy and the ASEA requirements alongside the schedule.

Does it cover dust areas as well as gas?

Yes. It is aimed at Zone 21 as well as Zone 1, which covers the coke handling, sulfur, cement, grain and mineral processing a gas-only specification leaves out. The dust marking is published with the certificate.
