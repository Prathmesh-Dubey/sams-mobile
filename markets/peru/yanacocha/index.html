The site · Yanacocha

Where an explosion proof mobile phone is needed at Yanacocha

Gold changes the hazard map. Yanacocha has operated since 1993 as a heap-leach operation, with ore stacked on leach pads, gold-bearing solution recovered through process plants and gold finished in a refinery. Newmont acquired Buenaventura's 43.65 per cent interest and then Sumitomo's remaining 5 per cent, which made it sole owner; it has described a sulphides project, with the Yanacocha Verde and Chaquicocha deposits, intended to extend operations towards 2040 and produce gold, copper and silver. That plan would add a conventional concentrating circuit to a site that was built on leaching. Most of what a person walks past at Yanacocha is therefore not a combustible-dust area. The areas to examine are reagent and fuel storage, carbon regeneration and refinery furnaces with their fuel supplies, and any new sulphide plant. Cyanide is a toxic-gas control problem, not an explosive-atmosphere one, and a handset's Ex marking does not answer it.

What Yanacocha does to a handset

Cajamarca sits at high altitude in the northern highlands, with frequent rain and cold. The environmental problem is moisture and cold more than dust. The IS-04Z1 is designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21 and has IP68 sealing; certification is in progress and no certificate has yet been issued.

Rain, fog and wet pads

The northern highlands are wetter than the southern desert mines, and heap-leach areas are wet by design. IP68 sealing is relevant to rain, spray and dropped-in-a-puddle events, though leach solution contact should be treated as a chemical exposure to avoid.

A narrower hazard map

Because much of the site is not a gas or dust zone, an Ex handset is needed in a smaller number of places. Procurement should ask where those places are and how many people work in them before sizing any roll-out.

Explosives and radio rules

Blasting areas have their own restrictions on transmitting devices. A managed handset lets the operator keep a policy for where radios and phones may be switched on, but the blasting rules come first.

A documentation-heavy approval culture

Yanacocha has operated under close public and regulatory attention for decades, including the Conga dispute. A complete, verifiable certificate trail matters more than speed.

A site with long-standing procedures

A mine running since 1993 has settled routines. New equipment tends to arrive through a formal trial, so plan evaluation as a documented exercise with defined success criteria.

Questions · Yanacocha

Frequently asked questions

Does a gold heap-leach mine need an explosion proof phone at all?

In some areas, yes, such as fuel and reagent storage and fuel-fired furnaces. Much of a leach operation is open-air and not classified, so the case is narrower than at a concentrator. The operator's classification study defines the boundary.

Is cyanide an explosive-atmosphere hazard?

No. Cyanide is a toxic-gas and chemical control problem, handled by gas detection and procedures. An Ex-rated handset does not protect against it, and no device claim here should be read that way.

Who owns Yanacocha now?

Newmont announced in 2022 that it had acquired the remaining interests from Buenaventura and Sumitomo, making it the sole owner and operator. Any later change should be checked against Newmont's own filings.

Does the northern climate change the device requirements?

Cajamarca has a wetter, cooler highland climate than the southern copper belt. Sealing and battery performance in cold and wet conditions are the points to test in any trial.
