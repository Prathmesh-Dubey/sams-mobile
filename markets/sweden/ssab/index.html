Sweden · SSAB

Explosion proof mobile phone for SSAB in Sweden

SSAB makes steel at Luleå, Oxelösund and Borlänge, and is changing how it makes it, with electric arc furnaces and hydrogen-based ironmaking in its published plans. This page is for the maintenance, process and HSE teams deciding what an explosion proof mobile phone has to do on those sites. The IS-04Z1 is designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21 and is still pre-launch, with ATEX certification in progress. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

The sites · Luleå, Oxelösund and Borlänge

Process gas and dust at SSAB Luleå, Oxelösund and Borlänge

An integrated steel plant is a gas works as well as a mill. Blast furnace gas is a toxic, flammable fuel gas that is piped around the site, and coke oven gas is rich in hydrogen. Luleå and Oxelösund make iron in blast furnaces and carry those gases, with coal and coke dust in the raw materials yards. Borlänge is a rolling and finishing site, where furnaces and annealing lines use fuel gas and sometimes hydrogen-bearing atmospheres. SSAB has published its plan to replace the blast furnaces with electric arc furnaces, working with LKAB and Vattenfall on hydrogen-based ironmaking through HYBRIT. Each step brings changes to the area classification drawings, and hydrogen is group IIC, which is why the IS-04Z1 targets IIC.

What a steel plant does to a handset

Steel plants are hot, loud, dusty and spread over large areas, and the Swedish ones add a cold winter. These conditions come up whenever steel maintenance teams talk about handsets.

Radiant heat and hot surfaces

Hot steel and furnace casings raise ambient temperature around an operator. The temperature class on the certificate (T4 is the target) and the certified ambient range are the two figures a plant engineer checks first.

Dust that gets into everything

Coal, mill scale and iron oxide dust settle on every surface. IP68 sealing and rugged build are the design answer, and Zone 21 is part of the target for coal dust areas.

Toxic gas that a phone cannot detect

Carbon monoxide from process gas is the major risk on a blast furnace site, and an intrinsically safe phone is not a gas detector. Keep the personal monitor and use the handset to report and call.

Contractors and rolling shutdowns

Mills stop on planned outages with large contractor crews. Android Enterprise enrolment lets a site issue and wipe handsets by contractor, and the existing MDM console manages them alongside the rest of the estate.

Work and approval

Outages, contractors and the approval step

Steel plants run continuously and stop for planned outages, when furnace relines and mill rebuilds bring large contractor crews on site. Between outages, operators do rounds on a fixed route, often outdoors and often alone, which makes a handset that can call and report a safety-relevant tool.

Questions · SSAB

Frequently asked questions

Does an intrinsically safe phone protect against carbon monoxide?

No. It prevents the phone from igniting a flammable atmosphere. It does not detect carbon monoxide or other toxic gases, so the personal gas monitor remains mandatory on a blast furnace site.

Why would a steelworks need IIC equipment?

Coke oven gas and the hydrogen planned for transition projects are hydrogen-rich, which is group IIC. Specifying IIC now avoids replacing handsets as hydrogen arrives.

Will the electric arc furnace change what is classified?

It changes the picture, since gas systems and coal handling shrink in some areas while hydrogen and electrical equipment grow in others. The site classification drawing decides the zone for any given location.
