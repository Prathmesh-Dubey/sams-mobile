The site · Sines

Explosion proof mobile phone for the Galp Sines refinery: what is classified

The Sines refinery is a deep-conversion plant rated at around 220,000 barrels a day. Its crude and atmospheric distillation units feed a fluid catalytic cracker and a hydrocracker, which Galp has described as processing about 43,000 barrels a day of heavy vacuum gasoil. Around those sit hydrogen supply, sulphur recovery, storage tanks and the jetties on the port side. Naphtha, LPG and light ends give gas-group IIA and IIB atmospheres; the hydrogen systems are group IIC; hydrogen sulphide is present wherever sour streams are handled. Dust is a minor concern here, so the working picture is Zone 1 and Zone 2 gas areas spread across a very large footprint.

Built for local conditions

What the Sines refinery does to a handset

The classification decides whether a device may be carried at all. After that, the layout and the shutdown calendar decide whether it is useful.

Distance between units

A walking round at a refinery of this size covers kilometres between the distillation area, the cracker, the tank farm and the jetties. The 4,000 mAh battery is specified to last a shift, because a charging point inside a classified area is not something an operator can count on.

A turnaround is a population event

When the plant stops, contractor numbers rise sharply and each person needs an accepted device. A handset managed from the operator's existing console, with Android Enterprise policies pushed in advance, is easier to issue and to recover than a pile of personal phones.

Atlantic exposure at the coast

Salt-laden wind, rain and wash-down are routine at the jetties and on the exposed structures. IP68 sealing is aimed at that environment, and the 50 MP camera records defects for the work order instead of relying on a description.

Photography is controlled

Refineries control cameras. The sensible answer is a camera that the operator's own console can disable by area or by user, which Android Enterprise management is designed to allow.

Use cases

Where the IS-04Z1 would be used at Sines

Operations01

Rounds through the process units

Operators record readings, photograph a leaking flange and call the control room from the same device. Four-constellation GNSS places the defect on the unit rather than in a verbal description.

Turnaround02

Shutdown crews and inspection teams

Each shutdown brings contractors who must be enrolled, briefed and badged within days. A single intrinsically safe handset model simplifies that process, and the console can lock it to the permit and inspection applications Galp has approved.

Hydrogen03

The new electrolyser area

Press reports describe a 100 MW electrolyser at the refinery due to enter operation in the second half of 2026. Hydrogen is group IIC, which is why IIC is the design target and not IIB.

Approvals and access

What Galp's HSE and procurement teams will ask for

The product requirement is the ATEX 2014/34/EU certificate and CE marking, with the marking string, temperature class and gas group stated so that the refinery's explosion protection document can record why the device suits each zone. Galp's own contractor and equipment onboarding is a separate step; we do not describe a programme name for it. Ask Galp's procurement and HSE teams what documents they need before a new device class is allowed through the gate.

Seveso upper-tier controls on a refinery usually add restrictions on ignition sources, photography and lone working. A handset that supports a managed camera policy and a lone-worker application helps with those, but the operator's rules remain the starting point. No certificate has been issued for the IS-04Z1, so no unit is supplied for use in a classified area until it exists.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Does Galp still refine at Matosinhos?

Galp announced that refining would stop at Matosinhos and concentrate on Sines, and press reports date the halt to October 2020. The site now handles storage and distribution, which is a different classified-area profile from a process plant, and a different conversation about handsets.

Would hydrogen from the new electrolyser change the equipment requirement?

It strengthens the case for group IIC equipment. Electrolyser halls and hydrogen compression are where an IIB-only device would be excluded. The IS-04Z1 is being certified to Ex ib IIC T4 as its marking target.

Can contractors bring their own phones into the refinery?

That is Galp's rule, not ours, and most refineries prohibit uncertified phones in classified areas. The usual solution is to issue an approved handset at the gate, which the operator's mobile device management enrols for the duration of the job.
