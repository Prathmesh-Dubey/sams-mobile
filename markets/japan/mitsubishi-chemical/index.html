Explosion proof mobile phone for Mitsubishi Chemical, Kashima

An explosion proof mobile phone for Mitsubishi Chemical sites in Japan, including the Kashima complex, is for operators who work among reactors, columns and storage inside classified areas. Mitsubishi Chemical is part of the wider Mitsubishi group of companies. This page is for the HSE, instrumentation and telecoms people who decide what may be carried past the gate at Kashima and the company's other plants. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

Why it fits

Designed for a keiretsu-affiliated petrochemical producer

Being part of the Mitsubishi group's network of affiliated companies shapes Mitsubishi Chemical's vendor relationships and engineering expectations in ways distinct from an independently operated petrochemical producer.

Keiretsu relationships can shape vendor introductions

Mitsubishi Chemical's affiliation with the wider Mitsubishi group of companies means new vendor relationships sometimes develop through group-level introductions or existing supplier relationships used elsewhere in the keiretsu, a different procurement dynamic from an independently operated chemical company.

Zone 1 gas at Kashima's cracker and reactor units

Kashima's olefins cracker and downstream reactor units are classified Zone 1 gas atmospheres, and Ex ib IIC T4 covers the hydrocarbon gas groups typical of that petrochemical service.

A shared industrial complex with other operators

Kashima hosts multiple companies' plants on adjacent infrastructure, meaning a device standard recognised at the complex level, not only by Mitsubishi Chemical, reduces friction for shared contractors and inspectors.

A long-operating, established petrochemical site

Kashima has been in operation for decades, meaning classified-area documentation reflects a similarly mixed-vintage layout to Japan's other major petrochemical complexes.

Fleet management within a large industrial group's framework

EMM and MDM enrolment supports Mitsubishi Chemical managing devices at Kashima and other sites within its own operational framework, distinct from wider group-level IT systems.

The sites

Explosion proof mobile phone use at Mitsubishi Chemical's Kashima site in Japan

Kashima, in Ibaraki Prefecture on the Pacific coast, is one of Japan's large petrochemical complexes and is shared with other operators, including a neighbouring refinery. A plant of this kind has olefin and aromatic process units, polymer plants, storage tanks and pipe racks spread over a wide area. Typical hazards are light hydrocarbon gas and liquid vapours, hydrogen, aromatics, solvents and combustible polymer powder, so both gas and dust zones appear. Operators, contractors and inspectors from several companies share roads and utilities, even though each company controls its own plant. Check the classification drawing for the specific plant.

Use cases

How work is organised in a shared chemical complex

Contractors01

Many companies, one road network

Inspection and maintenance firms move between neighbouring plants. A device with a clear marking and a tidy document set is easier to explain at several gates, though each company approves separately.

Turnaround02

Plant shutdowns

A cracker or polymer line stops on a planned cycle, and contractors flood in. Enrolled handsets can be issued with the permit and reporting apps already installed.

Quality03

Sampling and operator rounds

Operators sample at points around the plant and record the results. The handset records a photograph and a reading, and each entry carries a time and a GNSS position.

Frequently asked questions

Is the IS-04Z1 certified for use at Mitsubishi Chemical sites yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, and certification is still in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch.

Does keiretsu affiliation mean a different approval process?

Mitsubishi Chemical runs its own engineering and procurement function, though keiretsu relationships can sometimes influence how new vendor relationships are introduced.

Does approval at Kashima carry to other Mitsubishi Chemical sites?

Not automatically; each site's own engineering review should confirm applicability.

Does a device accepted at Kashima work at neighbouring companies' plants in the same complex?

No, each company operating at Kashima runs its own separate procurement process, even where sites are adjacent.

Which Mitsubishi Chemical plants might use an explosion proof phone?

Kashima is the site referenced here, and other plants may also have classified areas. Check the classification at each site with the site engineer.

Does the Mitsubishi group handle supplier approval for its companies?

Mitsubishi Chemical runs its own procurement and engineering function. Ask its procurement and HSE teams for the requirements. SAMS has not verified a specific programme and does not claim one.

Is combustible dust a concern at a petrochemical complex?

Yes, wherever polymer powder is stored and handled. The IS-04Z1 design target includes Zone 21, subject to the certificate once issued.

Do neighbouring plants accept the same handset?

Each operator reviews equipment itself, so acceptance at one plant does not transfer to the next.
