Class II locations get missed when a fleet is specified for gas alone. The Zone 21 design target covers dust: sulfur handling, coal transfer, solids charging.

Shifts on dispersed sites

Regulatory route · United States

What your HSE and procurement teams will ask for.

What your HSE and procurement teams ask for starts with the code. Hazardous locations are classified under the National Electrical Code, and the Division system, Class I Division 1 for gas and Class II Division 1 for combustible dust, is what most American sites still specify, even though NEC 505 and 506 also permit the international Zone system. Certification is done by a Nationally Recognized Testing Laboratory, and UL, FM Approvals and CSA are the marks buyers recognize.

The listing is not a formality. OSHA requires electrical equipment used in the workplace to be approved, which in practice means NRTL-listed, so the mark on the back of the handset is what the gate check looks for. Two approvals sit alongside it: FCC equipment authorization before any radio device is sold here, and MSHA permissibility under 30 CFR Part 23, which governs underground coal instead of the NEC. This program covers the NEC and FCC routes, not MSHA.

Ask about United States →
Is the IS-04Z1 Class I Division 1 approved today?

No. It is designed for Class I Division 1 and Zone 1 duty and the certification program is in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch. Until an NRTL certificate exists we will not call it listed.

Which NRTL will list it?

It is pursued through the recognized route for intrinsically safe handheld equipment. When the certificate issues we publish the laboratory, the mark, the file number and the conditions of safe use, so your safety officer can verify it.

Can it be used in an underground coal mine?

No. Underground coal falls under MSHA, where telephones and signaling devices need permissibility under 30 CFR Part 23, a scheme separate from the NEC and not part of this program. Surface mining under OSHA is in scope.

Does it work offshore in the Gulf of Mexico?

It is designed for that duty: Class I Division 1 and Zone 1 gas areas, IP68 and rugged build, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth where cellular coverage stops, and enterprise management for a rotating crew. Confirm your operator and BSEE requirements alongside the listing.

Will it have FCC authorization, and is there a Spanish interface?

FCC equipment authorization is part of the launch program and is required before the device can be sold or operated here; band support is confirmed in the datasheet at launch. Android 16 carries full Spanish interface support.

When can we order, and at what price?

Launch is Q4 2026. Until then you can register interest, request the datasheet, book an evaluation unit or take a pilot place. Pre-launch pricing is on request once we know fleet size and supply route.
