Poland · Refining and Petrochemicals

Explosion proof mobile phone for Refining and Petrochemicals in Poland

Poland’s refining and petrochemical hazardous areas are concentrated at Płock and Gdańsk, at the Orlen Południe sites in the south, and in the downstream plants alongside them. This page is for HSE, maintenance and telecoms teams at those sites and the contractors who work on their units, who need an explosion proof mobile phone in Poland for Zone 1 and Zone 21 work. The IS-04Z1 is designed for that; certification is in progress ahead of Q4 2026. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

The sector · Refining and petrochemicals

Explosion proof mobile phone for Polish refining and petrochemicals

This page takes the sector view: what each type of unit does to a handset, whoever owns it. For the operator’s organisation and sites, see explosion proof mobile phone for Orlen. Płock is both refinery and petrochemical plant, and Gdańsk is a refinery with a coking complex commissioned in the EFRA programme. Orlen Południe works with biocomponents at Trzebinia and with base oils and oil regeneration at Jedlicze. A refinery is not one hazard but a series: crude distillation, hydrotreating, conversion, sulphur recovery, olefins, polymers, loading. Each has its own gas group, its own temperature class and its own turnaround. The Płock complex is the larger of the two and carries the olefins and polymer chain; Gdańsk is a coastal site where crude, product and coke move by ship, pipeline and rail. Orlen’s subsidiary Anwil, at Włocławek, takes chemistry further into PVC, and the chemical plant view covers that side.

Unit types and where to find them

Crude distillation
Płock and Gdańsk; hot hydrocarbon liquid and vapour, gas group IIA.
Hydrotreating and conversion
Hydrogen at pressure; gas group IIC and a leak is hard to see.
Sulphur recovery
Hydrogen sulphide, toxic and flammable at once, so the personal monitor is the primary tool.
Coking
Gdańsk; heavy residue converted to coke, with coke dust in handling areas.
Olefins and polymers
Płock; ethylene, propylene and polyolefin powder.
Biocomponents and base oils
Trzebinia and Jedlicze; methanol and flammable liquids in smaller plants.
Flare, utilities and tank farms
Product and intermediate storage at both main sites, with flare headers that vent during upsets.

How the work is organised

Shift rounds
Operators walk fixed routes in each unit and record readings.
Turnaround
Planned shutdowns of one unit at a time, with contractor numbers many times the permanent staff.
Permit to work
Every job in a classified area runs under a written permit, which is where handset rules are set.
Contractor fleets
Mechanical, electrical and instrument contractors arrive with their own devices and procedures.
Marine and rail loading
Gdańsk jetty and rail racks, where vapour is released during filling.
Control rooms
Outside classification, but the point at which the handset gets its instructions.
Hot work and isolation
Jobs that break containment are issued only after isolation and gas testing, and the handset carries the permit, not the ignition source.

Hydrogen beside hydrocarbon

Gas group IIC covers hydrogen. The marking target is II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4, so a handset used in a hydrotreater unit is not limited to the hydrocarbon half of the plant.

Hydrogen sulphide needs a monitor

No phone replaces a personal H2S monitor. What the IS-04Z1 adds is an alarm record, a position and a photograph of the cause.

Dust where a unit has solids

Polyolefin powder and coke dust call for Zone 21. That is part of the same programme, so a round that crosses from vapour to powder does not need a second device.

A turnaround is a staffing event

During a shutdown, hundreds of contractors can arrive within days. Enrolment, restriction and wiping through EMM and MDM turns that into a procedure.

Cold and wet outdoors

IP68 sealing and a rugged build are aimed at outdoor work in winter, and the ambient range will be fixed by the certificate rather than promised in advance.

Position, not just a photograph

A refinery has thousands of tag numbers and many look alike. A GNSS position, four constellations, plus a photograph removes doubt about which flange the report refers to.

Questions · Refining

Frequently asked questions

Which gas groups does a refinery need?

Most hydrocarbon work is gas group IIA; ethylene is IIB; hydrogen is IIC. A handset marked IIC covers all three, but the temperature class and the zone still have to match each area.

Does Płock differ from Gdańsk for equipment suppliers?

The certificate does not, but each site keeps its own classification drawings, permit procedures and contractor induction. Ask each site what it needs, and do not assume that one site’s answer applies to the other.

Does a turnaround change the approval route?

Not the certificate, but the permit regime. Contractor equipment is usually reviewed by the operator’s HSE team before it is used, so ask what evidence they need.

Is this device approved for Polish refineries?

No. Certification is in progress, SAMS has no pilot or order at a Polish refinery, and the decision to allow it belongs to each operator.
