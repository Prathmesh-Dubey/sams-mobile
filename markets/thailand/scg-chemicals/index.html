Thailand · SCG Chemicals

Explosion proof mobile phone for SCG Chemicals in Thailand

SCG Chemicals runs naphtha crackers and polyolefin plants at Map Ta Phut in Rayong, which makes it one of the places in Thailand where an explosion proof mobile phone has to cover gas and polymer dust in a single building. This page is for SCGC operations, maintenance and HSE teams and the contractors on their shutdowns. The SAMS IS-04Z1 is designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21; it is pre-launch and certification is in progress. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

SCG Chemicals · the site

The SCGC crackers and polymer plants at Map Ta Phut

Trade press describes SCG as owning two crackers at Map Ta Phut: the Rayong Olefins Company (ROC) cracker, reported at about 800,000 tonnes a year of ethylene and 400,000 of propylene, and the Map Ta Phut Olefins Company (MOC) cracker, reported at about 1 million tonnes of ethylene and 500,000 of propylene. Their output feeds polyethylene, polypropylene and PVC plants, including Siam Polyethylene, Thai Polyethylene, Thai Polypropylene, Thai Plastics and Chemicals and HMC Polymers, with ROC's own HDPE and PP lines reported at 920,000 and 720,000 tonnes a year. These are press figures, not SCGC statements. The point for a handset is the route: furnace and quench gas at one end, reactor loops and extruders in the middle, and powder silos and bagging at the other, all inside the IEAT estate.

Zone 1 gas and Zone 21 dust in one route

A walk from a reactor bay to a silo hall crosses both. One device designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21 means the operator carries one unit, not a gas phone and a dust phone.

Group IIC across the cracker

Ethylene and hydrogen run through the cold section, so the marking target is II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4. A device with a lower gas group would be refused entry to part of the plant.

Fine powder on seals and ports

Polymer powder works into connectors and charging ports. IP68 sealing and rugged build answer dust and washdown, but the Ex marking is what decides entry.

Humid heat and monsoon rain

Rayong is humid all year with the southwest monsoon from May. Open pipe racks and furnace structures give no shelter during rain.

SCG Chemicals · working and approvals

How work is organised at SCGC, and what its teams would ask for

A cracker runs for years between turnarounds, and the contractor population spikes during them.

Turnarounds and polymer line stops

Planned stops for the crackers and individual polymer lines bring contractor crews into classified areas on a pass with a fixed window. The unit must be enrolled before the gate and cleared at the end of the job.

Operator rounds and contractors together

Permanent operators and temporary crews are in the same area, so the console profile should differ between them: permit app and camera for one, a narrower set for the other.

Vendor and equipment approval

We have not verified an SCGC approval programme for portable devices, so we do not describe one. Ask SCGC procurement and HSE for the approval steps and documents expected alongside the Ex certificate.

National file

TISI, the Department of Industrial Works, IEAT and NBTC each apply on top of the IECEx evidence. All are in progress, and SCGC would still review the device itself.

Frequently asked questions

Can SCGC buy an explosion proof mobile phone from SAMS today?

No. The IS-04Z1 is pre-launch with a Q4 2026 launch, and IECEx, ATEX and NEC 500 certification are in progress. The datasheet and certification schedule are available now.

Where do the cracker capacities on this page come from?

From trade press describing the Map Ta Phut crackers, not from SCGC. Use SCGC's own published figures for any engineering decision.

Why mention a 2024 fire at a Map Ta Phut tank?

Trade press reported that a pyrolysis gasoline tank fire at Map Ta Phut in May 2024 led SCG to declare force majeure on cracker output. Events like that keep tank and transfer areas under close review and are why site teams ask hard questions about anything carried into them.
