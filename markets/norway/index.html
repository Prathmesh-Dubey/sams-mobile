Built for Norwegian conditions

Most hazardous area handsets are specified against heat and dust. Norway asks the opposite question, and asks it on a rotation.

For evaluators in Norway, the IS-04Z1 explosion proof mobile phone is designed around these points: Winter, wet and salt; Handed over, not owned; Gloves and a full shift; Gas offshore, dust in Grenland; Norwegian on the device; A fix where coverage thins.

Winter, wet and salt

IP68 sealing and rugged build, which covers low temperature and humidity, suit an exposed deck or an outdoor round in January. The ambient range the certificate fixes is published with the datasheet, not promised in advance.

Handed over, not owned

A handset on a two-week rotation changes hands more often than it changes battery. Android 16 with EMM and MDM lets it be wiped, re-enrolled and reissued to the incoming crew from the beach.

Gloves and a full shift

Rounds are long and outdoors, and there is no charging point inside a classified area. The 4,000 mAh cell is sized for the whole shift with the camera and a work order application open.

Gas offshore, dust in Grenland

The gas marking target is II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4, and Zone 21 dust protection belongs to the same programme. That matters where a fertiliser plant puts a powder area metres from a gas one.

Norwegian on the device

The framework regulations say Norwegian shall be used as far as possible offshore. Android 16 carries a Norwegian interface, and the manual and declaration of conformity are produced in Norwegian alongside English.

A fix where coverage thins

4G LTE, Wi-Fi and four-constellation GNSS hold a position and a link across an installation network, a plant boundary or a coastal site where one operator is not enough.

Regulatory route · Norway

What your HSE and procurement teams will ask for.

The product side is shorter than people expect. Norway is not in the European Union, but the EEA Agreement makes ATEX 2014/34/EU Norwegian law, implemented through the regulation on equipment for use in potentially explosive atmospheres that DSB administers with Arbeidstilsynet. A notified body certificate and the CE marking are the basis for supply, with no national scheme on top. Offshore buyers usually want the IECEx certificate too, because that is the currency the shelf reads, and radio conformity sits under the Radio Equipment Directive with surveillance by Nkom.

The duty side is where Norway is genuinely different. Offshore, Havtil supervises safety and the working environment on the shelf, under the name it took on 1 January 2024 in place of Petroleumstilsynet. The regime is functional: the regulations set the goal, the guidance points to standards such as NORSOK and IEC 60079, and the operator documents that its own choice meets them. Onshore, Arbeidstilsynet takes the workplace side and the explosion protection document. The third gate is commercial, because operators look for a profile in Magnet JQS (formerly EPIM JQS), the qualification register shared by operators on the Norwegian shelf, before opening a technical evaluation.

Ask about Norway →
Is the IS-04Z1 already ATEX certified for use in Norway?

No. It is designed to ATEX 2014/34/EU for Zone 1 and Zone 21 and the programme is in progress, with IECEx alongside it. Launch is planned for Q4 2026, and nothing is supplied for classified-area use before the certificate covering it exists.

Norway is not in the EU. Is a separate national approval needed?

No. The EEA Agreement brings ATEX 2014/34/EU into Norwegian law and DSB administers it here, so a notified body certificate and the CE marking are the whole product-side requirement. No additional Norwegian scheme follows.

What is the price of an explosion proof mobile phone in Norway?

There is no list price before launch, and a supplier quoting one for an uncertified device is worth a second look. Pre-launch pricing is given against a stated quantity, configuration and support scope. Evaluation units are issued under a trial agreement, not sold.

Can it be used offshore on the Norwegian continental shelf?

That is one of the design cases. Zone 1 classification, IP68 sealing and rugged build are aimed at exposed decks, and the certificate evidence is written to go into the operator documentation Havtil supervises. Offshore trials are part of the pilot programme.

Will there be a Norwegian interface and Norwegian documentation?

Yes. Android 16 carries a Norwegian interface, and the manual, safe-use instructions and declaration of conformity are produced in Norwegian alongside English, which is what the framework regulations expect offshore.

How does it cope with cold and winter weather?

Construction is IP68 sealing and a rugged build, and the design assumes outdoor use rather than a heated control room. The ambient range fixed by the certificate is published on the datasheet; cold battery behaviour is what a Norwegian trial should measure.

Are you registered in Magnet JQS?

Registration is part of the launch programme here, because operators on the Norwegian and Danish shelf work from that register. Ahead of it, evaluation units and documentation come under a direct trial agreement.
