Explosion proof mobile phone for marine and offshore in Australia

An explosion proof mobile phone for marine and offshore work in Australia is needed where a ship, a jetty or a platform handles fuel or gas. This page covers the LNG jetties, bulk wharves, supply bases and offshore facilities that link Australian resource projects to their markets, and where the IS-04Z1 Zone 1 design target applies on the shore side of that work. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

The sector · Australia

Explosion proof mobile phone areas at Australian ports and offshore bases

Every large Australian resource project has a marine end. Woodside loads LNG, LPG and condensate at the Burrup, Chevron ships from Barrow Island and Onslow, INPEX loads at Bladin Point in Darwin Harbour, and the three Curtis Island plants load at Gladstone. Iron ore leaves from Port Hedland and Dampier, and coal from Hay Point, Abbot Point, Gladstone and Newcastle. Offshore support depends on bases at Dampier and Darwin, and Prelude is served by helicopters from Broome. Gas and fuel are the main hazard at the LNG berths and bunkering points, and the bulk wharves handle dust.

What the waterfront does to a handset

Marine work is wet, windy and metallic. The classified area is only one of the three problems, and often not the first one a device meets.

Spray, rain and wash-down

IP68 sealing keeps water out, and rugged build handles knocks on steel decks and gangways. Glove operation matters in wet and cold-water work.

Ship rules are separate

A vessel is governed by its flag state and class society rules. Those are separate from IECEx and ANZEx, so a vessel operator decides what may be carried aboard.

Shore side first

The IS-04Z1 Zone 1 design target applies to gas areas on jetties and bases. Do not assume it covers shipboard areas without the vessel operator's agreement.

Long gaps between charges

Jetty crews and support boat crews are far from safe-area charging. The 4,000 mAh battery is specified for a working shift.

Contractor-heavy sites

Supply bases and wharves are used by many companies at once. Android Enterprise management lets a base operator enrol and retire handsets per contract.

Work pattern and buying

How work is organised, and what buyers ask

Marine work is shared between terminal owners, port authorities, shipping lines and contractors, so more than one party may have a say in what handset is carried. SAMS makes no claim of any relationship with any operator named on this page.

Who decides
The terminal operator decides shore-side equipment. The vessel operator decides what goes aboard a ship. Ask both.
Shift and watch patterns
Port and vessel work runs round the clock, so handsets move between shifts and need a charge routine.
Supplier approval
Terminal and base operators set their own requirements. SAMS has not been through any such process; ask the procurement and HSE teams for current documents.
Evidence
IECEx certificate and scope, ANZEx from the same reports, and the ACMA RCM radio marking. NOPSEMA oversees offshore facilities in Commonwealth waters. All in progress ahead of Q4 2026.

Questions · Marine and offshore

Frequently asked questions

Is the IS-04Z1 certified for marine and offshore use in Australia yet?

No. The device is pre-launch, with IECEx and ANZEx certification in progress ahead of the Q4 2026 launch. No certificate has been issued.

Can it be used aboard a vessel?

Not on the basis of the IECEx or ANZEx certificate alone. A vessel's operator and class rules decide what handset may be used aboard.

Does it suit a helideck?

Helideck rules are set by the helicopter operator and the facility owner. Ask them before any handset is taken to a helideck.

Which sector pages overlap with this one?

The oil and gas and mining pages cover the operators whose terminals and wharves are listed here.
