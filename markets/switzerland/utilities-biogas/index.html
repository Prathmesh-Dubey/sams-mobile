The sector · Wastewater and biogas

Explosion proof mobile phone use at Swiss wastewater and biogas plants

Almost every canton has wastewater works with anaerobic digesters, and the gas they produce is a mixture of methane and carbon dioxide with hydrogen sulphide as a minor but important component. Methane is gas group IIA, so the group requirement is modest, but the zones are real: digester domes, gas holders, gas pipework, compressor rooms and flares are classified, and the zone depends on ventilation and the type of equipment. Agricultural and industrial biogas plants add feedstock reception and storage. Where the gas is upgraded to biomethane, membrane or scrubbing skids and the injection point to the gas grid are classified too. Dried sewage sludge, wood-based feedstock and similar materials can introduce a dust question. These sites are staffed by small teams, often alone, with outstations spread across a region, so the practical needs are lone working, remote photographs and a reliable network. The cantonal water-protection and energy departments and the gas industry association, SVGW, set the rules around them, and the plant's explosion protection document sets the zones.

What a digester site asks of a handset

Wastewater and biogas sites are small, wet and often unmanned at night. The handset has to work in those conditions.

Wet, corrosive surroundings

Digester galleries are humid and carry hydrogen sulphide. IP68 sealing is designed to resist water ingress, though chemical exposure should be assessed on each site.

Alone in the gallery

Many sites have one person on duty. A managed handset can run the applications that a lone-working policy needs, but the policy is the site's.

Remote sites and patchy coverage

Four-constellation GNSS and 4G LTE support location and connectivity; rural coverage should be checked.

Winter and ice on exposed plant

Digester roofs, gas holders and outdoor skids in the Swiss Plateau and the Alps are cold and often wet from autumn to spring. Battery performance falls in the cold on any device, so test the 4,000 mAh cell on a real winter round before relying on it for a full shift, and keep a spare charged in a safe area.

Records for the regulator

Photographs and timestamped inspection notes support the records that cantonal inspectors and insurers expect.

Questions · Utilities

Frequently asked questions

Is methane a harder gas group than solvents?

No. Methane is group IIA, less demanding than IIC. The IS-04Z1 marking target of IIC covers it, but the zones at biogas plants depend on ventilation and equipment.

What about hydrogen sulphide?

It is toxic as well as flammable, and a personal gas detector is the safeguard, not a phone. The handset does not replace one.

Why does a wastewater works need an Ex phone at all?

Because digester gas is flammable and a leak at a gas holder, a dome relief valve or a condensate trap can create a classified atmosphere where a personal phone is not designed to be carried. Operators decide which areas are classified, and the handset follows that decision.

Do small plants need a managed fleet?

Not necessarily, but a few devices in an MDM console is manageable, and the site decides.

Can it be used today?

No. Certification is in progress and no certificate has been issued.
