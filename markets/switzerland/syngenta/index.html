Switzerland · Syngenta

Explosion proof mobile phone for Syngenta in Switzerland

Syngenta makes crop protection products at Monthey in the Chablais and at Kaisten in Aargau, and both are chemical production sites in the full sense. This page is for the EHS, engineering and maintenance staff who need an explosion proof mobile phone in Switzerland for solvent and powder areas. The IS-04Z1 is pre-launch and in certification, and Syngenta has no relationship with SAMS. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

The sites · Monthey and Kaisten

Where an explosion proof mobile phone is needed at Syngenta in Switzerland

Syngenta describes Monthey as its largest production site in Switzerland, with more than 850 employees, making products from basic active ingredients through to final formulations. That range is the point: synthesis uses solvents and reactive intermediates, and formulation brings powders, granules and liquids. Monthey is also a shared chemical platform in the Chablais, where several producers sit within one fence and share site services, so contractors and staff from different companies cross each other's areas daily. Kaisten is a different animal. Syngenta calls it a fully automated site that supplies global demand for one key ingredient, S-Metolachlor, which it has invested in expanding. A highly automated plant has few operators, so those who do walk it are mostly doing inspection, sampling and maintenance. The hazards are solvent vapour in synthesis and storage, powder in formulation and packing, and flammable-liquid movement between tanks, all with a classification set by the site.

What a crop protection site asks of a handset

Chemical production in a valley or a highly automated plant creates specific demands. These are the ones that decide whether a handset is carried or left at the gate.

A plant with few people in it

On a highly automated site the operator on a round may be alone. A managed handset with a camera, location and a call to the control room, in a design aimed at Zone 1, is what makes a lone round workable. Lone-working policy remains the site's decision.

Shared sites, shared rules

At a multi-company site, equipment rules can differ by plot. The Ex marking target of II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4 is designed for the common gas groups, but each operator's own approval requirements still apply.

Weather that moves a round outdoors

Pipe racks, tank farms and loading areas are outside, in Alpine winters and wet summers. IP68 sealing is aimed at rain and washdown rather than only at dry dust.

Coverage in a valley

Four-constellation GNSS and 4G LTE are designed to hold a position and a connection in valley terrain, though coverage on a particular site should be checked during an evaluation.

Questions · Syngenta

Frequently asked questions

Which Syngenta site is more relevant to an Ex handset, Monthey or Kaisten?

Monthey, because it has a larger workforce and a broader range of solvent and powder work. Kaisten has fewer people, but the ones present are doing the inspection and maintenance rounds where a handset helps.

What would Syngenta ask of a supplier?

We have not been through a Syngenta supplier process and will not describe one. Ask the site's procurement and EHS teams for their equipment approval requirements and area classification documents, and for the rules of any shared-site operator, such as CIMO at Monthey.

Does Kaisten need a different approach from Monthey?

The questions are the same, but the numbers differ. Kaisten runs with a small team on a highly automated plant, so the case rests on lone working, remote photographs and reliable communication rather than on fleet size.

Does the handset support lone working?

It supports the managed applications and location services that a lone-working scheme could use, but SAMS does not supply a lone-worker alarm service. The scheme itself belongs to the site.
