The site · Shuaiba

What is classified at Shuaiba

The industrial area grew up around the Shuaiba refinery and the docks that served it. The refinery itself, Kuwait’s oldest, was commissioned in 1968, expanded to 200,000 barrels a day and shut permanently on 31 March 2017 as part of the Clean Fuels Project, with the site turned over to storage. What is left is more varied than a refinery and, for a portable device, harder. Petrochemical Industries Company runs fertiliser and heavy chemical production here. EQUATE operates steam cracking with ethylene oxide, ethylene glycol and polyethylene downstream of it, and Kuwait Olefins Company added the second cracker to the same complex in 2009. Around them sit the port, gas-fired generation and desalination. A single shift can move from a hydrocarbon unit that is Zone 1 gas into a polymer or fertiliser handling area that is Zone 21 dust, and back out to a jetty.

What Shuaiba does to a handset

The reason people ask about a device for this area rather than for Kuwait in general is that Shuaiba is not one hazard. It is gas, dust and salt within a few hundred metres of each other.

Dust classification is not an afterthought here

Polymer powder, pellets, fertiliser and bulk solids put Zone 21 across large parts of this area, and a device certified only for gas is not a lawful answer in those buildings. The IS-04Z1 is designed for Zone 21 as well as Zone 1, which is what lets one specification cover a plant and its bagging hall.

Cracker service means group IIC

Ethylene, hydrogen and ethylene oxide are not forgiving gas groups. The ATEX marking target is II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4, and IIC with a T4 temperature class is the combination an olefins plant will hold you to.

Coastal humidity, salt and summer heat together

Shuaiba sits on the water, so equipment gets salt and humidity on top of temperatures that run above 45 °C for months. IP68 sealing and rugged build are the specifications that matter to a device carried outdoors here all day.

Multi-employer sites and a mixed workforce

Plants at Shuaiba run with a mix of Kuwaiti staff and expatriate contractor crews. Android 16 carries Arabic and English interfaces on the same build, so one image can be issued across a crew instead of maintaining two.

Devices you do not own, on your permits

Contractor handsets are the awkward part of any petrochemical site. EMM and MDM enrolment lets you apply the camera policy and the application list to a device for the duration of a contract and take it off again at the end.

Questions · Shuaiba

Frequently asked questions

Can we order an ex proof phone for Shuaiba now?

Not yet. The IS-04Z1 is pre-launch with a Q4 2026 launch, and IECEx, ATEX and NEC 500 certification are all in progress. What is available today is the datasheet, a written certification schedule and an evaluation unit for handling and enrolment trials in safe areas.

What is the price of an ATEX phone in Kuwait?

There is no published price while certification is running, and no quoted lead time. Pre-launch pricing is given in writing against a stated quantity and configuration. If a supplier gives you a firm figure and a delivery date for an uncertified Ex device, ask to see the certificate number.

Do we need a Zone 21 rating for a petrochemical plant?

Wherever combustible dust is present in normal operation, yes: polymer powder and pellet handling, fertiliser bagging and bulk solids areas are commonly Zone 21 or Zone 22. A gas-only device does not cover them, which is why the IS-04Z1 is designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21 together.

Is the old Shuaiba refinery still operating?

No. It was shut permanently on 31 March 2017 as part of the Clean Fuels Project and the site was turned over to storage, with refining consolidated at Mina Al-Ahmadi, Mina Abdullah and Al-Zour. Storage tankage is still classified work even though the process units have gone.

Does the handset work in Arabic?

Yes. Android 16 runs the interface in Arabic or English on the same handset and the user manual will be issued in both. Your own plant applications keep whatever languages they already use.

Who has to approve it before it enters a plant?

The plant operator does, through its own engineering review, on top of the national conformity route. The certificates are what that review is built on. The KUCAS, IECEx and CITRA route into Kuwait is explained on the Kuwait page.
