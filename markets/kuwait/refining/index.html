Kuwait · Refining

Explosion proof mobile phone for refining in Kuwait

Kuwait refines about 1.4 million barrels a day at three refineries run by KNPC. This page is for the process, maintenance and telecoms teams who need an explosion proof mobile phone for Kuwait refining work, and it follows the process units rather than the company, which has its own page. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

The sector · Refining

Process units across three refineries

Mina Al-Ahmadi is rated at 346,000 barrels a day and Mina Abdullah at 454,000. The Clean Fuels Project integrated them into one complex of about 800,000 barrels a day, with Fluor managing the programme. Al-Zour, at 615,000 barrels a day, was reported fully commissioned in early 2024 and passed to KNPC with the KIPIC merger on 1 July 2026. The old Shuaiba refinery, commissioned in 1968, closed on 31 March 2017. Where a refinery is deep-conversion, the process shapes the hazard. Distillation gives hydrocarbon vapour, hydroprocessing gives hydrogen, the coker gives dust, and sulphur plants give sour gas. Products leave by sea: the Mina Abdullah sea island carries berths 18 and 19, while Mina Al-Ahmadi has its own crude and product export piers. Refinery work is organised around operators on shift rounds and periodic turnarounds that bring in contractor crews for a fixed window, so handset policy has to work for permanent staff and for crews who arrive with a work pack and leave when the scope is closed. Company-level detail is on the page for the explosion proof phone for KNPC, and site-level detail on Mina Abdullah and Ahmadi. Al-Zour is covered on the KIPIC page, since it was built and commissioned under that company.

Why refining shapes the specification of a Zone 1 mobile phone in Kuwait

Hydrogen, coke and a shutdown cycle that brings in thousands of contractors set refinery requirements apart from upstream or petrochemical sites.

Hydrogen puts group IIC on the specification

ARDS, hydrocracking and hydrogen plants rule out equipment certified only for IIA or IIB. The IS-04Z1 is designed to Ex ib IIC T4, with certification still in progress.

Coke dust alongside gas

The delayed coker's cutting and conveying produces combustible dust. A Zone 21 design target means one handset serves the gas and dust areas.

Shutdown cycles

A turnaround multiplies contractors and permits for a short window. An enrolled handset carrying permit app and directory reduces the kit each crew signs out.

Offshore berths

Berths and sea islands are reached over water, so ingress protection must be real rather than nominal. IP68 sealing is the design figure.

Use cases · Refining

Where the IS-04Z1 goes to work.

Operations01

Unit rounds

An operator walking the conversion units logs readings, photographs a leak and calls the panel inside the classified area. The round route is fixed, so a GNSS fix and a photograph on one record let the day shift see exactly what the night shift saw, without a radio call to describe it.

Turnaround02

Permit and isolation tracking

A permit office handling hundreds of crews can see who has been issued which isolation list from timestamped records. Handsets are issued at mobilisation, restricted to the approved application list for the contract, and recovered at demobilisation, so no crew leaves with an enrolled device or a stale permit.

Marine03

Product loading

A loading master works with ship and tank farm outdoors, in salt air and full sun. NFC reads at the arm and photographs of the connection run from one device, and the call to the control room needs no second radio.

Questions · Refining

Frequently asked questions

Is the IS-04Z1 approved for Kuwaiti refineries?

No. It is pre-launch and no certificate has been issued.

Why does a refinery need a dust-rated phone?

Coke handling at a delayed coker produces combustible dust, so those areas can be Zone 21 or 22.

Can the camera be disabled?

Yes, through the MDM console, which suits sites where photography is permit controlled.

Where are the company pages?

See the pages for KNPC, for KIPIC and Al-Zour, and for Mina Abdullah for a site-level view of the conversion units and sea island.

Does turnaround timing affect when to evaluate?

Yes. Safe-area handling and enrolment trials are best finished before a shutdown is planned, because during the window itself the permit and telecoms teams have no capacity to trial new equipment. Ask KNPC for the planning horizon rather than assuming one.
