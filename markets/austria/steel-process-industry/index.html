Austria · Steel and process industry

Explosion proof mobile phone for steel and process industry in Austria

Steel and process industry in Austria means two very different kinds of plant: integrated steelworks at Linz and Donawitz, and agricultural processing sites such as AGRANA's bioethanol and wheat starch works at Pischelsdorf. This page explains where an explosion proof mobile phone is used across those sites in Austria, and what the IS-04Z1, an intrinsically safe Zone 1 and Zone 21 phone in certification ahead of its Q4 2026 launch, is designed to do in each.

The sector · Austria

Where an explosion proof mobile phone is used in Austrian steel and process plants

The steel route is gas and heat: coke-oven gas, blast furnace gas and converter gas collected and distributed across the works, plus coal, coke and ore dust at handling points. The agricultural route is dust and alcohol. At Pischelsdorf in Lower Austria, AGRANA's site combines wheat starch with Austria's only bioethanol plant, and company sources give about 200 employees and a wheat processing capacity of 1.2 million tonnes after the second starch plant was added. Wheat, flour, starch and gluten are combustible dusts, and ethanol is a flammable liquid with vapour in gas group IIB. AGRANA also runs sugar and starch plants elsewhere in Austria, with the sugar processed in an autumn campaign. One sector label, two sets of hazards.

What steel and agriculture do to a handset

The two halves of this sector need the same handset to handle gas and dust, but in very different quantities and with very different seasons.

Hydrogen-rich gas in the steel route

Coke-oven gas is in gas group IIC, so the target marking II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4 fits it. The works' classification decides where.

Alcohol in the process route

Ethanol is gas group IIB, so the same IIC marking covers it. The more important point is temperature class, with ethanol well within T4.

Dust in both halves

Coal and ore dust in steel, flour and starch dust in food processing. The dust marking and maximum surface temperature on the certificate, once issued, are what the safety officer compares with the dust's ignition characteristics.

Seasonal and outage working

Sugar is processed in an autumn campaign with continuous shifts, and steel works run planned outages. The pattern in both cases is a short period with many contractors on site, which is when a managed fleet helps.

What operators in the sector would ask of a handset supplier

We have not found public handset supplier routes at voestalpine or AGRANA and claim none. Both are Austrian employers with explosion protection documents under the VEXAT, so a handset must come with data that the safety officer can enter in that document. Ask the procurement and HSE teams which supplier file and induction apply, and expect each site to decide separately.

Questions · Steel and process

Frequently asked questions

Is the IS-04Z1 certified for steelworks or starch plants?

No. Certification is in progress for a Q4 2026 launch, and we have no customer relationship with any operator named on this page.

Are steel dust and starch dust treated the same?

They are both combustible dusts, but their ignition characteristics differ. The safety officer compares the certificate's dust marking with the data for the actual dust.

Does the ethanol plant need a different handset from the starch plant?

The gas group and dust marking are covered by the same specification target, but each area has its own classification and the plant decides.
