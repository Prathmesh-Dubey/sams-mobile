Explosion proof mobile phone for Lenzing viscose fibre production

Lenzing in Upper Austria is the home site of a fibre producer that makes viscose, modal and lyocell from wood pulp. This page is for the plant, HSE and instrumentation teams who ask what an explosion proof mobile phone can and cannot do in a cellulose fibre works in Austria, and it is candid about one point: the IS-04Z1, an intrinsically safe Zone 1 and Zone 21 phone still in certification, currently targets temperature class T4, which matters a great deal next to carbon disulfide.

The site · Lenzing, Upper Austria

Where an explosion proof mobile phone is carried at Lenzing in Austria

Lenzing is described in public sources as the largest integrated pulp and viscose fibre production site in the world, and it is also the company's head office. Wood is turned into pulp, the pulp is converted to fibre, and the energy and chemical recovery systems behind both run on the same grounds, along with hydropower on the river. In the classic viscose and modal routes, cellulose is treated with carbon disulfide to form a soluble xanthate and then spun into an acid bath, which releases carbon disulfide and hydrogen sulfide into the spinning and recovery areas. Lyocell uses a different, closed-loop solvent system. Cut staple fibre and pulp sheets add cellulose dust and lint. So one site carries a very low ignition temperature gas, toxic gas and fibrous dust.

Carbon disulfide and temperature class

Because carbon disulfide ignites at a very low temperature, areas for it are normally specified at T6. The IS-04Z1's marking target is II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4, so as designed it is not a candidate for a carbon disulfide area. That is stated here so nobody finds out at a site audit.

Where the handset is useful

The same site has large areas that are not classified for carbon disulfide: wood handling, pulp and fibre dust areas, utilities, yards and tank farms. A Zone 21 and Zone 1 device suits those, and the plant's classification drawings decide each one.

Wet and chemical atmospheres

Spinning halls are humid and chemical. IP68 sealing protects the electronics, but the housing material and cleaning chemicals have to be checked against the process, so request that data before any trial and do not assume it.

Rounds across a very large site

Lenzing is a works with several production lines and long distances between them. Four-constellation GNSS and a battery specified for a shift suit foot or bicycle rounds, and the console can restrict applications by area.

What Lenzing's teams would ask of a handset supplier

We have not found a public supplier registration route at Lenzing and claim none. What the safety and engineering teams will ask is clear from the chemistry. The first question will be the temperature class on the certificate, and an honest answer decides at once which areas the device can enter. Procurement and HSE teams should say which supplier induction and which approval steps apply to equipment brought onto the site.

Questions · Lenzing

Frequently asked questions

Can the IS-04Z1 be used where carbon disulfide is present?

As designed, no. The target marking is T4, while carbon disulfide areas are normally T6. Certification is still in progress and the final certificate sets the scope, so Lenzing's engineers should rely on that and not on a design target.

Which parts of a fibre works could use it?

Areas not classified for carbon disulfide, such as dust areas, utilities and yards, subject to the plant's own classification. Each location needs a decision by the safety officer.

Is lyocell a different hazard from viscose?

It uses a different closed-loop solvent system, so the gas hazard is different from carbon disulfide. We do not state the details of the plant's classification, which belongs to Lenzing.
