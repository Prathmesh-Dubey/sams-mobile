Where it works · Austria

Where it works in Austria

Austria is landlocked and its hazardous areas are processing rather than production. Crude arrives by pipeline from Trieste, gas arrives from the east and leaves for Italy and Germany, and the heavy industry sits on the Danube and in the alpine valleys. A single working day can move from a Zone 1 pump house to a Zone 21 bagging hall without leaving the same company.

Operators and contractors such as OMV, Gas Connect Austria and Borealis need an explosion proof mobile phone they can carry into classified areas at Schwechat, Baumgarten an der March and Linz. The IS-04Z1 is designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21 duty there.

Regulatory route · Austria

What your HSE and procurement teams will ask for.

On the product side Austria is short and predictable. ATEX 2014/34/EU is transposed by the Explosionsschutzverordnung 2015, which took effect on 20 April 2016 and replaced the 1996 ordinance. A notified body issues the certificate, the manufacturer draws up the EU declaration of conformity and applies the CE marking, and that is the end of it: there is no separate Austrian product approval for equipment intended for potentially explosive atmospheres. Austrian buyers frequently see TÜV AUSTRIA on adjacent paperwork, and any European notified body certificate is equally valid here. Radio functions run under RED 2014/53/EU, with market surveillance handled by the Austrian telecoms authorities, RTR and the Fernmeldebehörde.

The employer side is where Austria differs from its neighbours, and it is a difference worth understanding before a German document set is copied across the border. Austria does not use the German BetrSichV. It uses the VEXAT, the Verordnung explosionsfähige Atmosphären, made under the ArbeitnehmerInnenschutzgesetz, which requires a specific explosion risk assessment, zone classification, and an Explosionsschutzdokument under section 5 that names the equipment and the reasoning behind it. The Arbeitsinspektion enforces this and publishes a commentary on the ordinance, and the AUVA issues practical guidance for it. In practice that means procurement asks for the certificate number, the full marking string and German documentation, then the safety officer decides whether the entry in the explosion protection document can be defended at the next inspection.

Is the IS-04Z1 already ATEX certified for Austria?

No. It is designed to ATEX 2014/34/EU for Zone 1 and Zone 21 and the certification programme is in progress, with IECEx running alongside it. Launch is planned for Q4 2026, and nothing is supplied for use in a classified area before the certificate covering it exists.

Does Austria require a national approval on top of ATEX?

No. The Explosionsschutzverordnung 2015 transposes ATEX 2014/34/EU directly, so a notified body certificate and the CE marking are the whole product-side requirement. There is no further Austrian scheme for Ex equipment to clear afterwards.

We already have a German BetrSichV file. Does it work in Austria?

Not unchanged. The product evidence transfers because ATEX is European, but the employer duty in Austria comes from the VEXAT rather than the BetrSichV, and section 5 of the VEXAT sets out what the Explosionsschutzdokument must contain. Groups running plants on both sides of the border normally keep one technical dossier and two compliance wrappers.

What will an explosion proof phone cost in Austria?

There is no list price before launch, and any supplier quoting one for a device still in certification is worth a second look. Pre-launch pricing is given against a stated quantity, configuration and support scope, in euros. Evaluation units are issued under a trial agreement rather than sold.

Will the documentation and the interface be in German?

Yes. Android 16 carries a German interface, and the manual, the safe-use instructions and the declaration of conformity are produced in German alongside English. Austrian safety officers and works councils treat that as a condition of acceptance, so it is planned into the launch.

Is it suitable for Zone 21 dust areas in a steel works or a starch plant?

Zone 21 is a design target of the same programme as Zone 1, because dust is the more common Austrian hazard once you leave refining. Coal and coke dust, wood and pulp dust and starch and sugar dust all belong to that category, and the certificate will state the dust marking and maximum surface temperature explicitly.

Can we join the pilot programme before launch?

Yes. Austrian operators, contractors and technical wholesalers can register interest now, receive the documentation, and take an evaluation unit under a trial agreement while certification continues. That is the normal route for a product still in its certification programme.
