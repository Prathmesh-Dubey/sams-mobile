Explosion proof mobile phone for chemicals, polymers and fibres in Austria

Chemicals in Austria cover polyolefins, cellulose fibres, antibiotics and chlor-alkali, and no two of them share a hazard. The sector · Austria

Where an explosion proof mobile phone is used in Austrian chemicals, polymers and fibres

The useful way to read this sector is by substance, not by company. Polyolefin plants at Schwechat and Linz handle ethylene, propylene, hydrogen and polymer powder. Lenzing's fibre works handle carbon disulfide, hydrogen sulfide and cellulose dust. Sandoz at Kundl handles flammable solvents and pharmaceutical powders. A chlor-alkali plant such as Donau Chemie's at Brückl in Carinthia produces hydrogen as a by-product of electrolysis alongside chlorine. The gas group, the temperature class and the dust marking differ in each, and an honest supplier tells each plant where its product fits and where it does not.

What chemical plants do to a handset

The common thread is temperature class: a handset is useful in a plant only if its T class is below the lowest ignition temperature in the area.

T4 is the number to check

T4 means a maximum surface temperature of 135 degrees Celsius. That suits most hydrocarbons and many solvents, but not carbon disulfide, which is why Lenzing's page is blunt about it.

IIC is the broad gas group

Hydrogen sets the gas group to IIC. A device marked for IIC is not limited by it in polyolefin, chlor-alkali or solvent areas, but the certificate defines the exact scope.

Process safety is wider than ignition

Toxic gas such as chlorine and hydrogen sulfide is a separate hazard. A phone is not a detector, and personal monitors stay mandatory.

Many contractors, many sites

A chemical park has dozens of tenants. A managed handset with per-area policy lets one contractor team work across plants without carrying different devices.

Questions · Chemicals and fibres

Frequently asked questions

Is one certification enough for all these plants?

The certificate defines the scope. Each plant then compares it with its own classification, so the same device may be suitable for one area and not for another.

Why does the page say the device is not for carbon disulfide?

Because the target temperature class is T4, while carbon disulfide areas are normally T6. Certification is in progress and the final certificate sets the scope.

Is the IS-04Z1 certified?

No. Certification is in progress ahead of a Q4 2026 launch, and we have no customer relationship with any operator named here.
