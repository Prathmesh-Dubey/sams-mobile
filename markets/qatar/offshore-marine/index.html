The sector · Offshore and marine

Explosion proof mobile phone for Qatar's offshore and marine sites

Qatar's offshore work falls into three groups. Oil is produced at fields such as Al Shaheen, operated by North Oil Company, and the facilities at Halul Island handle offshore crude storage and tanker loading. Gas comes from the North Field platforms, which supply Ras Laffan. And a marine layer ties them together: supply vessels, tankers and LNG carriers at the berths of Ras Laffan and Mesaieed, with helicopters moving crews. Dolphin Energy's export line adds a subsea pipeline from Ras Laffan to the UAE. Almost everyone in this sector arrives by boat or helicopter and leaves on a schedule, so what they carry has to work for the whole stay. Field detail for Al Shaheen is on the North Oil Company page.

What the sea does to a handset

Salt, heat and distance shape every offshore requirement. A handset that fails mid-rotation costs far more than the handset.

Salt air that never lets go

Offshore equipment is exposed to salt in the air and to spray during transfers. IP68 sealing and rugged build, including salt fog, are the specified response. Rinsing the unit down after a transfer is part of the routine.

Replacement by boat

A damaged handset means a delivery on the next scheduled boat. For that reason we recommend evaluating a unit through a complete rotation, and why repair runs through SAMS and its Qatari partner.

A different rulebook on vessels

A tanker or carrier follows shipboard regulations and the terminal's own. A handset that has hazardous-area certification is not automatically accepted on board. The vessel's master and the terminal decide.

Carriage by helicopter

Operators of helicopter flights set their own rules on batteries and electronic devices. Check them against the 4,000 mAh battery before sending devices offshore.

How work is organised

Rotations, transfers and approval offshore

Offshore and marine work follows a timetable of arrivals and departures, and the handset has to fit inside it.

Crew change
Rotations end with a handover. Notes, photographs and positions on an enrolled device travel with the shift log rather than staying with the person who wrote them.
Cargo transfer
Loading is timed with a vessel alongside. The jetty head is far from the control building, so reporting from the berth matters.
Connectivity
Coverage depends on platform and terminal networks. Applications that tolerate poor links are more useful than ones that need constant data.
Marine classification
Some offshore equipment must also satisfy marine or offshore classification. Settle the scope with the operator's engineers once certificates exist.
Vendor approval
Ask the operator's procurement and HSE teams what applies. National conformity and radio approval for Qatar are in the launch programme and have not been granted.

Questions · Offshore and marine

Frequently asked questions

Is the IS-04Z1 certified for Qatar offshore use yet?

No. It is pre-launch, with a Q4 2026 launch date and certification in progress. No certificate has been issued, and operators decide on their own review once one exists.

Does it need additional marine equipment approvals?

Some offshore equipment must meet marine classification requirements as well as ATEX or IECEx. The scope for a handset is confirmed with the operator alongside the certificates.

Can it go aboard a tanker or LNG carrier?

That is the vessel master's decision and depends on shipboard rules and the terminal's requirements, not only on the handset's certificate.

Can it be used on a helicopter flight?

Carriage of electronic devices and lithium batteries on flights is set by the aviation operator. Ask them before you send a fleet.
