Explosion proof mobile phone for ADNOC Offshore platforms

ADNOC Offshore's fields are worked from Das Island, Zirku Island and the artificial islands of Upper Zakum, where there is no shop and no spare handset within reach. This page is for the HSE officers, platform telecoms teams and marine contractors who need an explosion proof mobile phone for offshore Abu Dhabi that survives salt air and long rotations. The IS-04Z1 is designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21 gas and dust areas and is being certified ahead of its Q4 2026 launch; it has no certificate yet. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

The sites · ADNOC Offshore

Offshore Abu Dhabi: Das Island, Zirku and Upper Zakum

Offshore production in Abu Dhabi is run through two operating companies, ADMA-OPCO and ZADCO. ADMA-OPCO works from Das Island, where crude from fields such as Umm Shaif and Lower Zakum is processed, stored and exported. ZADCO works from Zirku Island, about 140 km north-west of Abu Dhabi, which receives crude from Upper Zakum, Umm Al Dalkh and Satah and has two single point mooring berths for tankers. Upper Zakum itself is produced from four artificial islands built in shallow water roughly 84 km offshore, which means drilling, processing and accommodation all sit on reclaimed ground with a boat or helicopter as the only way in. Many of these facilities handle live gas with the sea on every side, so classified areas run right up to the edge of the deck.

What the Gulf does to a handset

Offshore Abu Dhabi combines salt, humidity and isolation in a way inland plant never does. Four points decide whether a handset is still useful on the next rotation.

Salt that never dries out

Humidity on the Gulf coast is high through the year and every surface carries salt film. IP68 sealing and rugged build are specified for permanent exposure, so ports, buttons and charging contacts are the parts to inspect, not the housing.

A spare is a transfer away

If a unit fails on an island, the replacement arrives on the next boat or helicopter. A 4,000 mAh battery that covers a shift and a sealed body are specified to keep a unit in service until then. Ask the helicopter operator how it treats portable devices and spare batteries before planning a fleet.

Hand-overs that carry evidence

Crew change is where information is lost. A handset that stores readings, located photographs and shift notes with four-constellation GNSS positions gives the next rotation a record instead of a conversation.

Sour gas needs a separate instrument

Offshore sour gas development, including the SARB deep gas project, adds hydrogen sulphide to the hazard picture. Certification covers ignition, not toxicity, so personal H2S monitors remain mandatory. The handset can pair with a detector over Bluetooth; it does not replace one.

The approval path

Two gates for ADNOC Offshore

The national gate is MoIAT's ECAS-Ex scheme, which is built on IECEx certification; IECEx certification and ECAS registration are both in progress for the IS-04Z1, and the radio needs TDRA approval separately. The second gate is ADNOC's own supplier registration, pre-qualification and In-Country Value scoring. Offshore equipment can also face marine or platform-specific requirements beyond hazardous-area certification. We have not seen the criteria that ADMA-OPCO or ZADCO apply to handheld devices, so ask their procurement and HSE contacts rather than assume.

Questions · ADNOC Offshore

Frequently asked questions

Is the IS-04Z1 approved for ADNOC Offshore platforms?

No. National certification is a prerequisite for ADNOC's own review, and neither is complete. SAMS has no approval, order or relationship with ADMA-OPCO, ZADCO or ADNOC Offshore; this page describes design intent only.

Does it need marine-specific approvals beyond ATEX and IECEx?

Possibly. Some offshore equipment carries additional marine or installation requirements set by the operator. The scope will be confirmed alongside the IECEx and ATEX certificates, and your HSE team should state what the platform adds.

Can it be carried on an offshore helicopter?

That is the helicopter operator's decision, not the phone's. Operators publish rules on portable electronic devices and lithium batteries, so check them with logistics before a pilot is planned.
