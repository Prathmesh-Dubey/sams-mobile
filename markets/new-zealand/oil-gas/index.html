Explosion proof mobile phone for oil and gas in New Zealand

New Zealand's oil and gas industry is concentrated in Taranaki, and the choice of an explosion proof mobile phone there is shaped by a mature basin, a small number of operators and sites that range from offshore platforms to a fuel import terminal in Northland. This page is for the HSE, engineering and telecoms teams of those operators and their contractors, and it describes what the IS-04Z1 is designed for as it approaches its Q4 2026 launch. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

The sector · Taranaki and beyond

Who works where in New Zealand oil and gas

The upstream business is a handful of operators sharing one basin. OMV operates the Māui gas field off South Taranaki with its onshore production station at Oaonui, the Pohokura field with its production station at Motunui, and the Maari oil field 80 kilometres offshore; see our page on an explosion proof phone for OMV New Zealand. Todd Energy runs the Kapuni, McKee and Mangahewa fields onshore, covered on the Todd Energy page. Genesis Energy is a gas-field operator through Kupe. Downstream, Methanex's Motunui methanol plant has been the basin's largest gas customer, but it announced on 1 September 2026 that it will idle in the first quarter of 2027; read the Methanex page for the effect. Beyond Taranaki, Channel Infrastructure runs the fuel import terminal at Marsden Point, with two deep-water jetties, 280 million litres of tankage and a 170-kilometre pipeline to Wiri in South Auckland, according to its own description.

What a mature basin asks of an explosion proof mobile phone in New Zealand

Taranaki's fields are past their peak, and production decisions are driven by supply, price and regulation. For a handset, that means working in a business that is careful with money and exacting about safety.

Offshore transfers set the weight budget

Platforms such as Māui and Maari are reached by helicopter or vessel from Taranaki, so weight and battery life are considered before the first valve. The 4,000 mAh cell is specified to cover a shift without a recharge point in the area.

Remote operation, brief visits

OMV operates Pohokura remotely from New Plymouth, so a visit to site is for a defined task. A call, a photograph and a position fix back to the control room are what the visit needs, and the handset provides all three.

Condensate, crude and methane

Gas, condensate and crude vapours are generally group IIA. Ex ib IIC T4 is the design target, which covers IIA to IIC on a single marking, so one handset serves every operator's sites.

Contractors move between operators

Inspectors and service crews work for several companies in the same basin. A managed handset can be enrolled and wiped per job, so that each operator keeps control of its own policy.

Evaluating a Zone 1 mobile phone in New Zealand oil and gas

We do not have relationships with any operator named on this page and cannot describe their approval processes. Each runs its own, so ask each operator's procurement and HSE teams what a supplier of equipment for classified areas must provide.

Certificate evidence
IECEx Certificate of Conformity for Zone 1 gas, IIC and T4; none has been issued and certification is in progress.
Major hazard facilities
WorkSafe regulates major hazard facilities in New Zealand, and OMV says the Ōmata Tank Farm is one, so documentation there tends to be more formal.
ATEX alongside IECEx
International operators may ask for both; ATEX certification is also in progress.
Radio and service
A New Zealand partner is the usual route for service, spares and radio compliance.
Questions · Oil and gas

Frequently asked questions

Which operators does this page cover?

OMV, Todd Energy and Genesis Energy upstream, Methanex downstream and Channel Infrastructure in fuel import. Each has its own approval route, and none has been approached on our behalf.

Does declining gas supply change what is needed?

It changes the business more than the rating. The same Zone 1 requirement applies whether a field is ramping up or being run down, though project budgets are tighter.

Does the same device suit platform and terminal?

The design target is Zone 1 and Zone 21 for both, but each operator classifies areas separately and decides what may be carried.

Is there a price for a Taranaki operator?

No list price exists. Pre-launch quotes are written for a stated quantity and configuration once the zone, gas group and volume are known.
