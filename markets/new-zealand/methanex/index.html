New Zealand · Methanex

Explosion proof mobile phone for Methanex, Taranaki

Methanex is a Canadian methanol producer, and its Motunui site in Taranaki is where an explosion proof mobile phone for New Zealand methanol work would be carried. The position matters first: on 1 September 2026 Methanex announced it will sell substantially all of its New Zealand gas entitlements and indefinitely idle Motunui in the first quarter of 2027. This page is for the HSE, engineering and shutdown teams who will still be working in that plant, and the contractors planning around them. The IS-04Z1 is designed around intrinsic safety rather than a flameproof enclosure.

The site · Methanex

What is classified at Motunui

Methanex has run a Taranaki methanol business for decades, and its recent history there is a story of shrinking gas supply. A plant at Waitara Valley was idled indefinitely in the first quarter of 2021, one of the two Motunui trains was idled in September 2024, and Methanex reports New Zealand output of 0.5 million tonnes in 2025 against 0.7 in 2024. What remains is a single-train plant whose process is conventional and fully classified: natural gas is reformed to synthesis gas rich in hydrogen and carbon monoxide, converted to methanol, distilled, stored in tanks and loaded for export. Methanol vapour is gas group IIA and burns with a flame that is hard to see in daylight; the synthesis gas at the front end is a hydrogen-rich IIC mixture. The site is at Motunui, close to OMV's Pohokura production station on Lower Otaraoa Road, which is how gas reaches it from the basin.

What a methanol plant being put to sleep does to a handset

Most questions in this sector assume a plant that runs. Motunui is about to be run down, purged and preserved, which is a different kind of work with a different handset load.

A fire you cannot see

Methanol burns with a nearly invisible flame, so detection and procedure carry more weight than eyesight. A handset in a classified area must not be an ignition source, which is what Ex ib IIC T4 intrinsic safety is for.

Purging, draining and isolation

Methanex said it will operate the plant for several months and then idle and preserve it. Draining, purging and isolating a plant is paperwork-heavy, and a photograph of each isolation next to its permit is evidence that survives a handover.

Quieter plant, longer walks

With sections out of service, rounds cover long distances with few people about. Four-constellation GNSS puts a position on the record, and the 4,000 mAh battery is specified for a shift with no recharge in the area.

Coastal Taranaki weather

Wind, rain and salt come with the location. IP68 sealing and rugged build suit a handset that spends its shift outside.

Questions · Methanex

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth evaluating if Methanex is idling Motunui?

That is your judgement. A preserved plant still needs safe inspection, and other Taranaki gas and process sites share its hazards. The device is not specific to one operator.

Is the Waitara Valley plant still operating?

No. Methanex idled Waitara Valley indefinitely in the first quarter of 2021 because of insufficient gas supply, so a handset plan for Taranaki methanol is a Motunui plan.

Why does the gas group matter for a methanol plant?

Methanol vapour is IIA, but hydrogen in synthesis gas is IIC, the most demanding group. A device designed for IIC, as the IS-04Z1 is, covers the whole plant on one specification.

Does the idling change the classification?

Not by itself. Classification depends on what is in the equipment, so a purged and blanketed system may be treated differently from a live one. That is the plant's engineers' decision.
