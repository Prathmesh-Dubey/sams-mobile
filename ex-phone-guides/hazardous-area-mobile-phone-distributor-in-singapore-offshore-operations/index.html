The term · hazardous area

What “hazardous area” means for a smartphone

A hazardous area is a place where flammable gas, vapour or combustible dust may be present. Standards divide these into zones: Zones 0, 1 and 2 for gas and vapour, and Zones 20, 21 and 22 for dust. Zone 1 and Zone 21 are the tier where an explosive atmosphere is likely to occur in normal operation, which is where the IS-04Z1 is designed to work. Anything carried in must be marked for the zone, gas group and temperature class of the area.

No certificate has been issued for the IS-04Z1 yet, so it must not be taken into a classified area until the certificate covering it exists and the site operator has accepted it.

In Singapore offshore support sector. Hazardous areas here arise at FPSO and rig conversion, offshore equipment repair workshops, vessel mobilisation quays and supply base storage. Operators and yards classify them, and the picture changes as projects start and finish. SAMS does not classify areas, so a specifier should ask each project's safety team.

Frequently asked questions

Can we buy a hazardous area mobile phone in Singapore Offshore Operations today?

No. Nothing can be bought or taken into a classified area today: certification is in progress and launch is planned for Q4 2026. What is available now is the datasheet, the written certification schedule and evaluation units for safe-area use.

What does a hazardous area mobile phone in Singapore Offshore Operations cost?

SAMS has not published a price. Any figure you are offered for an uncertified Ex phone should be treated with caution; written indicative pricing is available for a defined quantity and site.

Which hazardous areas is the IS-04Z1 designed for?

Zone 1 and Zone 21 (gas and dust) with gas group IIC and temperature class T4, subject to certification. It must not be used in a classified area before its certificate is issued and the site has accepted it.

Which areas at Singapore offshore support sector would use it?

Likely candidates are rig and floating-unit conversion, offshore equipment repair workshops, vessel mobilisation quays, hazardous materials storage. Which areas are classified, and to what zone, is set by the site operator's own area classification drawings.

How do I become a SAMS distributor for Singapore offshore support sector?

Apply through the distributor page with your company details and the territory you cover. SAMS reviews applications company by company and has not yet granted a territory for Singapore offshore support sector.

How can I plan equipment for project cycles in offshore yards?

Newbuild and conversion projects ramp up in phases, with contractor numbers peaking during outfitting and commissioning. Dates are set by owners and yards, so contact the project team early to learn the approved-equipment list and induction requirements.

Do offshore support bases have strict access rules?

The Loyang base describes 24-hour manned security, strict access requirements and video surveillance. Expect advance registration and gate screening, and declare any electronic equipment. Confirm details with the host company before travel.

Who sets the equipment rules for offshore conversion projects?

Buyers here are typically yards, service companies and operators with their own approvals, and a project may follow the unit owner's rules as well. SAMS cannot grant approval. Ask each project's safety team for its device requirements, and confirm with the site operator.
