The term · industrial

What “industrial” means for a smartphone

“Industrial” describes a phone built for plant conditions: sealed, drop-resistant and manageable at fleet scale through Android Enterprise. It does not by itself mean the phone may enter a classified area. Only a device with a valid explosion-protection certificate and marking may do that.

The IS-04Z1 is designed to be both an industrial Android phone (IP68, 4,000 mAh battery, 50 MP camera, Android 16 with EMM and MDM support) and an intrinsically safe one. Its explosion-protection certificates are in progress and have not been issued.

In Edmonton refineries (Refinery Row). A compact industrial cluster means contractors and service firms are close by and often move between sites. That favours central EMM or MDM enrolment, with clear device ownership and return procedures. The IS-04Z1 is designed for Android 16 management, and charging would be organised in safe areas away from process units.

Frequently asked questions

Can we buy an industrial mobile phone in Edmonton Refinery today?

Not yet. The IS-04Z1 is pre-launch and its certification is still in progress ahead of a Q4 2026 launch. No unit goes into a classified area before the certificate covering it exists. The datasheet, the certification schedule and safe-area evaluation units are available now.

What does an industrial mobile phone in Edmonton Refinery cost?

No price is published. While certification is in progress there is no list price and no committed lead time, and a supplier quoting a firm figure for an uncertified Ex handset is promising something it cannot stand behind. Indicative pre-launch pricing is issued in writing against a stated quantity and site.

Is the IS-04Z1 an industrial phone as well as an Ex phone?

Yes by design: IP68 sealing, a 4,000 mAh battery, a 50 MP camera and Android 16 with EMM and MDM support. The explosion-protection certificates are separate and still in progress.

Which areas at Edmonton refineries (Refinery Row) would use it?

Likely candidates are refinery process units, crude and product tank farms, loading racks, wastewater treatment. Which areas are classified, and to what zone, is set by the site operator's own area classification drawings.

How do I become a SAMS distributor for Edmonton refineries (Refinery Row)?

Apply through the distributor page with your company details and the territory you cover. SAMS reviews applications company by company and has not yet granted a territory for Edmonton refineries (Refinery Row).

Is Edmonton hazardous-area work seasonal?

Cold winters affect outdoor work, and refineries run planned turnarounds outside peak demand. Dates are operator-set, so ask the operator's contractor team about shutdown windows and which devices are approved for process areas.

Which refineries are in the Edmonton area?

Refinery Row in Sherwood Park hosts the Strathcona (Imperial) and Suncor Edmonton refineries, and the Scotford and Industrial Heartland cluster has upgrading and refining capacity. Confirm which operator and site you will visit, because device rules are set per site.

Is a different approval needed for each refinery at Refinery Row?

Possibly. Equipment is normally approved by the site operator's HSE or electrical authority, and each operator runs its own process. Imperial Oil and Suncor may ask for different documents or trials. A single manufacturer certificate would help, but ask each operator what it requires before moving a device between sites.
