Field notes · South Korea

More about South Korea that matters for portable equipment

South Korea: industry on the ground

Ulsan is the industrial centre of the south-east, hosting the world's largest car assembly plant, the world's largest shipyard and the world's third-largest oil refinery, which makes it a dense mix of process and manufacturing hazards.

South Korea: geography and layout

The country has no proven oil reserves; the Donghae-1 gas field produced between 2004 and 2021 and east-coast gas exploration started in 2024, so most hazardous-area work is downstream rather than upstream.

South Korea: infrastructure

Thermal generation provides more than two thirds of electricity and nuclear power most of the rest; the first plant, Kori near Busan, opened in 1977, so large plant outages involve many contractors.

South Korea: industry on the ground

GS Caltex concentrates its refining and petrochemical plants at the Yeosu Complex, covering about 6 million square metres, with 800,000 barrels per stream day of capacity and Korea's largest heavy oil upgrading unit at 275,000 barrels a day.

The term · explosion proof

What “explosion proof” means for a smartphone

“Explosion proof” is the phrase most buyers use for equipment that may be taken into a classified hazardous area. Strictly, it covers several protection concepts: a flameproof enclosure (Ex d) contains an internal explosion, while intrinsic safety (Ex i) limits electrical energy so that an ignition cannot start. Phones are normally intrinsically safe, because a flameproof handset would be too heavy to carry all shift.

The IS-04Z1 uses intrinsic safety, with a marking target of Ex ib IIC T4, and is designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21. It is pre-launch, certification is in progress and no certificate has been issued.

In South Korea. Explosion-proof is the phrase used for flameproof equipment in Korea, but the IS-04Z1 relies on intrinsic safety. Flameproof Ex d contains a blast inside a heavy enclosure, while intrinsic safety limits the energy in a handheld. At Ulsan or Geoje the distinction decides whether a phone can enter refinery or tank areas.

Questions · South Korea

Frequently asked questions

Can we buy an explosion proof mobile phone in South Korea today?

No. Nothing can be bought or taken into a classified area today: certification is in progress and launch is planned for Q4 2026. What is available now is the datasheet, the written certification schedule and evaluation units for safe-area use.

What does an explosion proof mobile phone in South Korea cost?

SAMS has not published a price. Any figure you are offered for an uncertified Ex phone should be treated with caution; written indicative pricing is available for a defined quantity and site.

Is the IS-04Z1 explosion proof or intrinsically safe?

It is intrinsically safe (marking target Ex ib IIC T4), which is the usual way to make a phone for hazardous areas. It is not a flameproof (Ex d) design, and certification is still in progress.

Which sites in South Korea does this cover?

The market guide for South Korea covers Ulsan, Yeosu, Daesan, Onsan, among others. Site-level pages are linked below.

Is intrinsically safe the same as explosion proof?

Not exactly. Explosion proof is a broad everyday term; intrinsic safety is one protection concept, which limits energy so ignition cannot occur. Flameproof (Ex d) is a different concept that contains an explosion. The IS-04Z1 is intrinsically safe by design.

Where are the main hazardous-area clusters in South Korea?

Ulsan is the best-known, with a very large refinery, petrochemical works and shipyard, and the country's LNG import terminals are another cluster. Because supply is almost entirely imported, fuel storage and terminal sites are central; confirm each site's classification with its operator.

Do intrinsically safe phones need Korean certification for use on site?

Operators may ask for Korea's own national certification alongside, or instead of, international certificates such as IECEx or ATEX. Requirements differ by site and contractor, so ask the operator's safety team which marking and approvals it accepts before ordering devices.

Is the IS-04Z1 meant for shipyard tank work at Geoje?

The same Zone 1 specification is designed to cover tank-entry and marine-fuel hazards alongside refinery risk. Certification is in progress and no certificate exists. Confirm the exact area, gas group and permit rules with the yard's safety officer before any use.
