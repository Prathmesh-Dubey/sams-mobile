Field notes · KNPC

More about KNPC that matters for portable equipment

KNPC: background

Mina Al-Ahmadi dates from 1949 and Mina Abdullah was built in 1958, so equipment and layouts range from original plant to recent upgrades.

KNPC: geography and layout

Mina Al-Ahmadi covers about 10.5 million square metres, 40 km south of Kuwait City, and Mina Abdullah about 7.8 million square metres, 46 km south, so both are very large sites.

KNPC: people and language

KNPC sponsors a refinery-operations specialisation at the College of Technological Studies of the Public Authority for Applied Education and Training, creating a local technician pool.

KNPC: rules and approvals

Kuwait's communications regulator, CITRA, extended type approval validity from one to three years, with renewal timing set in advance, so handset certificates need diary control.

The term · IECEx

What “IECEx” means for a smartphone

IECEx is the international scheme for certifying equipment used in explosive atmospheres. A certificate of conformity is issued by an accredited certification body against a specific edition of the IEC 60079 standards and carries a schedule of conditions of safe use. It is not a legal requirement in most countries on its own, but many national schemes accept it as the technical basis.

The IECEx certificate for the IS-04Z1 is in progress and has not been issued. The marking target is Ex ib IIC T4, the intrinsic safety concept for Zone 1 gas with the hardest gas group (IIC) and a 135 °C temperature class.

In KNPC. KNPC's refineries host international EPCs and licensors, who typically work from IECEx documents. Their reviewers look for a certificate of conformity and its schedule of conditions. The IS-04Z1 is targeting IECEx, which remains in progress, so KNPC staff should treat it as a planned route rather than an approval already held.

Questions · KNPC

Frequently asked questions

Can KNPC buy the IS-04Z1 today?

No. Nothing can be bought or taken into a classified area today: certification is in progress and launch is planned for Q4 2026. What is available now is the datasheet, the written certification schedule and evaluation units for safe-area use.

What does an IECEx mobile phone for KNPC cost?

There is no list price yet. SAMS gives indicative pre-launch pricing in writing, against a stated quantity and site, and does not commit to a lead time before certification completes.

Does the IS-04Z1 hold an IECEx certificate?

No. The IECEx certificate is in progress and has not been issued. SAMS will publish the number when it exists.

How does KNPC approve suppliers?

Supplier registration and equipment approval are run by the organisation itself; confirm current requirements with its procurement and HSE teams.

Is intrinsically safe the same as explosion proof?

Not exactly. Explosion proof is a broad everyday term; intrinsic safety is one protection concept, which limits energy so ignition cannot occur. Flameproof (Ex d) is a different concept that contains an explosion. The IS-04Z1 is intrinsically safe by design.

Why are KNPC refinery units of different ages relevant?

Mina Al-Ahmadi was first built in 1949 and Mina Abdullah in 1958, and both have been upgraded, including ultra-low-sulphur diesel production from 2014. Older and newer areas can have different classification and equipment requirements, so request the current area classification drawings.

What happened to the old Shuaiba refinery?

It was shut in March 2017 so that its facilities could be repurposed for the Clean Fuels Project. Shuaiba is therefore mainly a decommissioning and conversion site, while active refining work is concentrated at Mina Al-Ahmadi, Mina Abdullah and Al-Zour; check current status with KNPC.

Can a phone be used around tank farms and LPG plants?

Only if its protection suits the zone and gas group in the operator's drawings. A device designed around intrinsic safety would be assessed against them, and LPG and fuel vapours call for the right group. The IS-04Z1 target is IIC, but certification is in progress. Ask KNPC's HSE team first.
