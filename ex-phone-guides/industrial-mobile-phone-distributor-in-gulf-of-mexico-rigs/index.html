The term · industrial

What “industrial” means for a smartphone

“Industrial” describes a phone built for plant conditions: sealed, drop-resistant and manageable at fleet scale through Android Enterprise. It does not by itself mean the phone may enter a classified area. Only a device with a valid explosion-protection certificate and marking may do that.

The IS-04Z1 is designed to be both an industrial Android phone (IP68, 4,000 mAh battery, 50 MP camera, Android 16 with EMM and MDM support) and an intrinsically safe one. Its explosion-protection certificates are in progress and have not been issued.

In US Gulf of Mexico offshore platforms and rigs. Platform crews work in rotations and depend on helicopter and vessel schedules, so devices should be enrolled in MDM or EMM before they travel. Connectivity offshore differs from onshore, so offline use and controlled return matter. Charging should be in the accommodation or other safe areas, not on the process deck.

Frequently asked questions

Can we buy an industrial mobile phone in Gulf of Mexico Rigs today?

No. Nothing can be bought or taken into a classified area today: certification is in progress and launch is planned for Q4 2026. What is available now is the datasheet, the written certification schedule and evaluation units for safe-area use.

What does an industrial mobile phone in Gulf of Mexico Rigs cost?

No price is published. While certification is in progress there is no list price and no committed lead time, and a supplier quoting a firm figure for an uncertified Ex handset is promising something it cannot stand behind. Indicative pre-launch pricing is issued in writing against a stated quantity and site.

Is the IS-04Z1 an industrial phone as well as an Ex phone?

Yes by design: IP68 sealing, a 4,000 mAh battery, a 50 MP camera and Android 16 with EMM and MDM support. The explosion-protection certificates are separate and still in progress.

Which areas at US Gulf of Mexico offshore platforms and rigs would use it?

Likely candidates are drill floors and well bays, production process decks, wellhead areas, utility and compression areas. Which areas are classified, and to what zone, is set by the site operator's own area classification drawings.

How do I become a SAMS distributor for US Gulf of Mexico offshore platforms and rigs?

Apply through the distributor page with your company details and the territory you cover. SAMS reviews applications company by company and has not yet granted a territory for US Gulf of Mexico offshore platforms and rigs.

How does the Gulf's lightning and storm season affect equipment planning?

The Gulf has frequent lightning and a hurricane season, and operators routinely shut in and evacuate platforms ahead of storms. Keep devices stowed and charged when weather is forecast, and follow the platform's procedures for electronics during storm warnings.

Who sets the safety rules for equipment on US Gulf platforms?

Federal offshore safety is overseen by the Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement, and each operator adds its own electrical and area-classification requirements. Before bringing any portable device offshore, confirm with the operator what approvals and markings they accept.

Who regulates safety on Gulf platforms?

The Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement is the lead federal agency for safety and environmental oversight on the Outer Continental Shelf. Operators and rig contractors also approve equipment against their own safety case. Confirm requirements with the operator before sending any device offshore.
