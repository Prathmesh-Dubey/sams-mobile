The term · Ex-proof

What “Ex-proof” means for a smartphone

“Ex-proof” is shorthand for equipment carrying an explosion-protection marking (the Ex mark) that matches the area it enters. The marking states the protection concept, gas or dust group and temperature class, and it must be backed by a certificate issued by a recognised body, so a phone described as Ex-proof should come with a certificate number you can check.

The IS-04Z1 is designed to carry the marking II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4. That is a target: certification is in progress, no certificate has been issued, and SAMS will publish the certificate numbers when they exist.

In US Gulf of Mexico offshore platforms and rigs. A marking check on a platform would compare the area classification drawings with the label, covering zone or division, gas group and temperature class. Wellhead and process areas handle hydrocarbon gas, and IIC marking gives margin. The IS-04Z1 target marking is II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4, pending certification.

Which areas at US Gulf of Mexico offshore platforms and rigs would use it?

Likely candidates are drill floors and well bays, production process decks, wellhead areas, utility and compression areas. Which areas are classified, and to what zone, is set by the site operator's own area classification drawings.

How does the Gulf's lightning and storm season affect equipment planning?

The Gulf has frequent lightning and a hurricane season, and operators routinely shut in and evacuate platforms ahead of storms. Keep devices stowed and charged when weather is forecast, and follow the platform's procedures for electronics during storm warnings.

Who sets the safety rules for equipment on US Gulf platforms?

Federal offshore safety is overseen by the Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement, and each operator adds its own electrical and area-classification requirements. Before bringing any portable device offshore, confirm with the operator what approvals and markings they accept.

Who regulates safety on Gulf platforms?

The Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement is the lead federal agency for safety and environmental oversight on the Outer Continental Shelf. Operators and rig contractors also approve equipment against their own safety case. Confirm requirements with the operator before sending any device offshore.
