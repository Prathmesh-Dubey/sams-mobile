The term · explosion proof

What “explosion proof” means for a smartphone

“Explosion proof” is the phrase most buyers use for equipment that may be taken into a classified hazardous area. Strictly, it covers several protection concepts: a flameproof enclosure (Ex d) contains an internal explosion, while intrinsic safety (Ex i) limits electrical energy so that an ignition cannot start. Phones are normally intrinsically safe, because a flameproof handset would be too heavy to carry all shift.

The IS-04Z1 uses intrinsic safety, with a marking target of Ex ib IIC T4, and is designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21. It is pre-launch, certification is in progress and no certificate has been issued.

In Peru. At Camisea or Talara people say explosion proof, but the concepts differ. Flameproof Ex d encloses an explosion and suits fixed equipment, while the IS-04Z1 uses intrinsic safety, Ex ib. That fits a handset carried through a gas processing plant or refinery rather than mounted on a pipe, and Zone 21 matters at concentrators.

Questions · Peru

Frequently asked questions

Can we buy an explosion proof mobile phone in Peru today?

Not today. Until the certificates are issued the IS-04Z1 stays out of classified areas, and until launch there is nothing to purchase. You can ask for the datasheet and the certification schedule, or book a safe-area evaluation unit.

What does an explosion proof mobile phone in Peru cost?

No price is published. While certification is in progress there is no list price and no committed lead time, and a supplier quoting a firm figure for an uncertified Ex handset is promising something it cannot stand behind. Indicative pre-launch pricing is issued in writing against a stated quantity and site.

Is the IS-04Z1 explosion proof or intrinsically safe?

It is intrinsically safe (marking target Ex ib IIC T4), which is the usual way to make a phone for hazardous areas. It is not a flameproof (Ex d) design, and certification is still in progress.

Which sites in Peru does this cover?

The market guide for Peru covers Cuajone / Toquepala, Antamina, Cerro Verde, Las Bambas, among others. Site-level pages are linked below.

How do I become a SAMS distributor for Peru?

Apply through the distributor page with your company details and the territory you cover. SAMS reviews applications company by company and has not yet granted a territory for Peru.

Which Peruvian authority approves mobile phones and radios?

MTC, the Ministerio de Transportes y Comunicaciones, runs type approval for radio equipment, and a testing partner reports that it is based on FCC standards with no local laboratory tests, no local representative requirement and no extra label. Confirm current requirements with MTC or a local agent.

Where do hazardous areas arise in Peruvian gas and mining operations?

Typically at the Camisea wells and plants in the Amazon basin, the Malvinas separation plant, the Pisco terminal, refineries on the coast, and mines with diesel storage, explosives and dust. Each operator issues its own classification, so request the zone drawing and gas or dust group.

Can the device cope with altitude and UV at mine sites?

The device is designed with an outdoor-readable display and rugged sealing, but ambient and altitude limits are fixed by the certificate, which is not yet issued. Compare the published range with your mine's conditions and confirm with the operator before use at sites above 4,000 metres.
