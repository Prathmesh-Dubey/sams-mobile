The term · explosion proof

What “explosion proof” means for a smartphone

“Explosion proof” is the phrase most buyers use for equipment that may be taken into a classified hazardous area. Strictly, it covers several protection concepts: a flameproof enclosure (Ex d) contains an internal explosion, while intrinsic safety (Ex i) limits electrical energy so that an ignition cannot start. Phones are normally intrinsically safe, because a flameproof handset would be too heavy to carry all shift.

The IS-04Z1 uses intrinsic safety, with a marking target of Ex ib IIC T4, and is designed for Zone 1 and Zone 21. It is pre-launch, certification is in progress and no certificate has been issued.

In Saint John, New Brunswick. At a large refinery, explosion-proof normally means flameproof Ex d equipment on fixed plant. A phone uses intrinsic safety. Process units, crude and product storage and the marine terminal are different hazard settings, and Irving Oil would define which protection concept is acceptable in each.

Frequently asked questions

Can we buy an explosion proof smartphone in Saint John Refinery today?

Not today. Until the certificates are issued the IS-04Z1 stays out of classified areas, and until launch there is nothing to purchase. You can ask for the datasheet and the certification schedule, or book a safe-area evaluation unit.

What does an explosion proof smartphone in Saint John Refinery cost?

Is the IS-04Z1 explosion proof or intrinsically safe?

It is intrinsically safe (marking target Ex ib IIC T4), which is the usual way to make a phone for hazardous areas. It is not a flameproof (Ex d) design, and certification is still in progress.

Which areas at Saint John, New Brunswick would use it?

Likely candidates are refinery process units, crude and product storage, marine terminal. Which areas are classified, and to what zone, is set by the site operator's own area classification drawings.

How do I become a SAMS distributor for Saint John, New Brunswick?

Apply through the distributor page with your company details and the territory you cover. SAMS reviews applications company by company and has not yet granted a territory for Saint John, New Brunswick.

Which areas of the Saint John refinery typically need Ex equipment?

Process units, tank farms, loading and marine terminal areas commonly have Zone 1 or 2 gas areas. The refinery's classification drawings define gas group and temperature class, so request them before specifying handheld devices.

What is Canaport and how is it relevant?

Canaport is a crude oil receiving terminal with a single-point mooring and an LNG terminal near Saint John. Marine and tank areas bring flammable vapour risks, so confirm the terminal's classification and device rules with the operator.

Who decides whether a phone is allowed in the marine terminal?

Irving Oil, as operator, decides which equipment is permitted in each area, normally through its HSE or electrical authority. Ship and tanker crews may have their own requirements too. Ask the refinery for its zone data and approval process, and do not assume a manufacturer certificate alone will be enough.
