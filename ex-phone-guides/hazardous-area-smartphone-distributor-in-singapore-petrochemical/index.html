The term · hazardous area

What “hazardous area” means for a smartphone

A hazardous area is a place where flammable gas, vapour or combustible dust may be present. Standards divide these into zones: Zones 0, 1 and 2 for gas and vapour, and Zones 20, 21 and 22 for dust. Zone 1 and Zone 21 are the tier where an explosive atmosphere is likely to occur in normal operation, which is where the IS-04Z1 is designed to work. Anything carried in must be marked for the zone, gas group and temperature class of the area.

No certificate has been issued for the IS-04Z1 yet, so it must not be taken into a classified area until the certificate covering it exists and the site operator has accepted it.

In Singapore petrochemical sector. Classified areas in this sector are cracker process units, chemical derivative plants, tank farms and polymer handling and dust areas. Each plant owner classifies its own areas, and the island has more than 100 companies. SAMS does not classify, so a specifier should obtain the relevant plant's documents.

Frequently asked questions

Can we buy a hazardous area smartphone in Singapore Petrochemical today?

Not today. Until the certificates are issued the IS-04Z1 stays out of classified areas, and until launch there is nothing to purchase. You can ask for the datasheet and the certification schedule, or book a safe-area evaluation unit.

What does a hazardous area smartphone in Singapore Petrochemical cost?

No price is published. While certification is in progress there is no list price and no committed lead time, and a supplier quoting a firm figure for an uncertified Ex handset is promising something it cannot stand behind. Indicative pre-launch pricing is issued in writing against a stated quantity and site.

Which hazardous areas is the IS-04Z1 designed for?

Zone 1 and Zone 21 (gas and dust) with gas group IIC and temperature class T4, subject to certification. It must not be used in a classified area before its certificate is issued and the site has accepted it.

Which areas at Singapore petrochemical sector would use it?

Likely candidates are cracker process units, chemical derivative plants, tank farms, polymer handling and dust areas. Which areas are classified, and to what zone, is set by the site operator's own area classification drawings.

How do I become a SAMS distributor for Singapore petrochemical sector?

Apply through the distributor page with your company details and the territory you cover. SAMS reviews applications company by company and has not yet granted a territory for Singapore petrochemical sector.

Who sets rules for equipment entering these plants?

Plant operators set the detailed rules, within workplace safety duties enforced by MOM and flammable-material duties under SCDF. Many require pre-approval of electronic devices, so provide model, certificate and zone details before the visit.

Are turnarounds a good time to introduce new field devices?

Turnarounds bring many contractors and tight schedules, and devices should be approved well before they begin. Dates are set by each operator, so ask the site's turnaround planner for the calendar and the equipment-approval lead time.

Does the IS-04Z1 marking cover polymer dust areas?

The target marking, II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4, is a gas marking. Polymer handling and dust areas are described as Zone 21, which is a dust classification and needs a separate dust-rated marking. The device has no certificate yet. Ask the plant which areas apply.
